<author id="b476-9">Danhof, C. J.</author>
<p id="AIi"><em>(dissenting). </em>Plaintiffs brought this action against defendants alleging negligent sterilization and breach of contract. The unsuccessful sterilization resulted in an unwanted pregnancy and an unplanned child. The jury in this case was instructed, according to the holding of <em>Troppi v Scarf, </em><span class="citation" data-id="2185063"><a href="/opinion/2185063/troppi-v-scarf/#255" aria-description="Citation for case: Troppi v. Scarf">31 Mich App 240, 255</a></span>; <span class="citation" data-id="2185063"><a href="/opinion/2185063/troppi-v-scarf/" aria-description="Citation for case: Troppi v. Scarf">187 NW2d 511</a></span> (1971), that the benefits, if any, to these plaintiffs from the unplanned child were to be weighed against all <page-number citation-index="1" label="465">*465</page-number>the elements of claimed damage. See also <em>Stephens v Spiwak, </em><span class="citation" data-id="2183335"><a href="/opinion/2183335/stephens-v-spiwak/#650" aria-description="Citation for case: Stephens v. Spiwak">61 Mich App 647, 650-651</a></span>; <span class="citation" data-id="2183335"><a href="/opinion/2183335/stephens-v-spiwak/" aria-description="Citation for case: Stephens v. Spiwak">233 NW2d 124</a></span> (1975). The plaintiffs affirmatively and voluntarily disclaimed the cost of raising the child as an element of their damages. No convincing argument has been made that this voluntary tactic should operate to preclude application of the <em><span class="citation" data-id="2185063"><a href="/opinion/2185063/troppi-v-scarf/" aria-description="Citation for case: Troppi v. Scarf">Troppi</a></span> </em>benefits rule. The <em><span class="citation" data-id="2185063"><a href="/opinion/2185063/troppi-v-scarf/" aria-description="Citation for case: Troppi v. Scarf">Troppi</a></span> </em>rule was intended to afford juries necessary flexibility in assessing damages. The majority opinion greatly impairs that flexibility.</p>
<p id="b477-4">Application of the <em><span class="citation" data-id="2185063"><a href="/opinion/2185063/troppi-v-scarf/" aria-description="Citation for case: Troppi v. Scarf">Troppi</a></span> </em>benefits rule would not have foreclosed recovery by these plaintiffs had they sought compensation for all their damages. See <em>Green v Sudakin, </em><span class="citation" data-id="1720144"><a href="/opinion/1720144/green-v-sudakin/" aria-description="Citation for case: Green v. Sudakin">81 Mich App 545</a></span>; <span class="citation" data-id="1720144"><a href="/opinion/1720144/green-v-sudakin/" aria-description="Citation for case: Green v. Sudakin">265 NW2d 411</a></span> (1978). If the majority opinion is correct that application of the <em><span class="citation" data-id="2185063"><a href="/opinion/2185063/troppi-v-scarf/" aria-description="Citation for case: Troppi v. Scarf">Troppi</a></span> </em>benefits rule "would * * * outlaw a cause of action” on the facts of the instant case, that is a consequence of the plaintiffs’ tactical decision to seek recovery for only a portion of their potential damages. I cannot agree that this is a sufficient reason for rejection of the <em><span class="citation" data-id="2185063"><a href="/opinion/2185063/troppi-v-scarf/" aria-description="Citation for case: Troppi v. Scarf">Troppi</a></span> </em>benefits rule.</p>
<p id="b477-5">The majority would allow these plaintiffs to split their cause of action thereby enabling them to recover for pregnancy damages without any offset for the benefits, if any, of the unplanned child. I would follow <em><span class="citation" data-id="2185063"><a href="/opinion/2185063/troppi-v-scarf/" aria-description="Citation for case: Troppi v. Scarf">Troppi</a></span>: </em><page-number citation-index="1" label="466">*466</page-number>nomic costs of an unplanned child in applying the 'same interest’ rule. Accordingly, the benefits of the unplanned child may be weighed against all the elements of claimed damage.” <span class="citation" data-id="2185063"><a href="/opinion/2185063/troppi-v-scarf/#255" aria-description="Citation for case: Troppi v. Scarf">Troppi, <em>supra, </em>at 255</a></span>. (footnotes omitted).</p>
