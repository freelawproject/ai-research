11  a daily or weekly basis, illegally parked his luxury high-end vehicles outside of Defendant

14  establishments, mainly the neighboring Carino’s Restaurant and Verde’s El Ranchero.”

18    B. Fake Negative Reviews                                                
19       On August 12 and 24, 2021, Plaintiff Thakore used Plaintiff DMM “to post more 
20  than  a  hundred  fake  negative  reviews  against  [Defendant  APM],  its  employees,  and 
21  [Defendant] Melone personally on Google and Yelp.”  (Id. ¶ 11.)  Many of the reviews 
22  “originated from [Plaintiff DMM] and employees within the company or included [Plaintiff 
23  DMM’s] company logo in their review.”  (Id.)  The reviews caused Defendant APM’s 
24  Google rating to drop from 5 to 2.9 stars during its busy summer season.  (Id.)  Before

21                          II.  LEGAL STANDARD                               
22       Federal Rule of Civil Procedure 12(c) allows parties to move for judgment on the 
23  pleadings after the pleadings have been closed but prior to trial, and “within such time as 
24  not to delay the trial.”  Fed. R. Civ. P. 12(c).  The standard for determining a Rule 12(c) 
25  motion for judgment on the pleadings is the same as the standard for a Rule 12(b)(6) motion 
26  to dismiss.  Cafasso, U.S. ex rel. v. Gen. Dynamics C4 Sys., Inc., </pre><span class="citation" data-id="213165"><a href="/opinion/213165/cafasso-v-general-dynamics-c4-systems-inc/" aria-description="Citation for case: Cafasso v. General Dynamics C4 Systems, Inc.">637 F.3d 1047</a></span><pre class="inline">, 1053 &amp; 
27  n.4 (9th Cir. 2011) (“Rule 12(c) is ‘functionally identical’ to Rule 12(b)(6) and that ‘the 
28  same standard of review’ applies to motions brought under either rule.”) (quoting Dworkin 
1  v. Hustler Magazine Inc., </pre><span class="citation" data-id="518481"><a href="/opinion/518481/andrea-dworkin-priscilla-moree-judith-fouts-v-hustler-magazine-inc/#1192" aria-description="Citation for case: Andrea Dworkin Priscilla Moree Judith Fouts v. Hustler...">867 F.2d 1188, 1192</a></span><pre class="inline"> (9th Cir. 1989)).  “Judgment on the 
2  pleadings is proper when the moving party clearly establishes on the face of the pleadings 
3  that no material issue of fact remains to be resolved and that it is entitled to judgment as a 
4  matter of law.”  Hal Roach Studios, Inc. v. Richard Feiner and Co., Inc., </pre><span class="citation" data-id="537184"><a href="/opinion/537184/hal-roach-studios-inc-a-delaware-corporation-v-richard-feiner-and/" aria-description="Citation for case: Hal Roach Studios, Inc., a Delaware Corporation v....">896 F.2d 1542</a></span><pre class="inline">, 
5  1550 (9th Cir. 1989).  A court must not consider matters beyond the pleadings; otherwise, 
6  such a proceeding must properly be treated as a motion for summary judgment.  Id.; see 
7  also Yakima Valley Mem’l Hosp. v. Wash. State Dep’t of Health, </pre><span class="citation" data-id="223637"><a href="/opinion/223637/yakima-valley-memorial-hospital-v-washington-state-department-of-health/" aria-description="Citation for case: Yakima Valley Memorial Hospital v. Washington State...">654 F.3d 919</a></span><pre class="inline">, 925 n.6 
8  (9th Cir. 2011) (“Judgment on the pleadings is limited to material included in the pleadings 
9  … [o]therwise, the proceeding is converted to summary judgment.”) (citations omitted). 
10       “A motion to dismiss under Federal Rule of Civil Procedure 12(b)(6) for failure to 
11  state a claim upon which relief can be granted ‘tests the legal sufficiency of a claim.’” 
12  Conservation Force v. Salazar, </pre><span class="citation" data-id="221641"><a href="/opinion/221641/conservation-force-v-salazar/" aria-description="Citation for case: Conservation Force v. Salazar">646 F.3d 1240</a></span><pre class="inline">, 1241–42 (9th Cir. 2011) (quoting Navarro 
13  v. Block, </pre><span class="citation" data-id="6998032"><a href="/opinion/7092494/navarro-v-block/#732" aria-description="Citation for case: Navarro v. Block">250 F.3d 729, 732</a></span><pre class="inline"> (9th Cir. 2001)).  An action may be dismissed for failure to 
14  allege “enough facts to state a claim to relief that is plausible on its face.”  Bell Atl. Corp. 
15  v. Twombly, </pre><span class="citation" data-id="9435068"><a href="/opinion/145730/bell-atlantic-corp-v-twombly/#570" aria-description="Citation for case: Bell Atlantic Corp. v. Twombly">550 U.S. 544, 570</a></span><pre class="inline"> (2007).  “A claim has facial plausibility when the plaintiff 
16  pleads factual content that allows the court to draw the reasonable inference that the 
17  defendant is liable for the misconduct alleged.  The plausibility standard is not akin to a 
18  ‘probability requirement,’ but it asks for more than a sheer possibility that a defendant 
19  acted unlawfully.”  Ashcroft v. Iqbal, </pre><span class="citation" data-id="9435339"><a href="/opinion/145875/ashcroft-v-iqbal/#678" aria-description="Citation for case: Ashcroft v. Iqbal">556 U.S. 662, 678</a></span><pre class="inline"> (2009) (internal citations omitted).  
20  For purposes of ruling on a Rule 12(b)(6) motion, the Court “accept[s] factual allegations 
21  in the complaint as true and construe[s] the pleadings in the light most favorable to the 
22  nonmoving party.”  Manzarek v. St. Paul Fire &amp; Marine Ins. Co., </pre><span class="citation" data-id="1194077"><a href="/opinion/1194077/manzarek-v-st-paul-fire-marine-insurance/" aria-description="Citation for case: Manzarek v. St. Paul Fire &amp; Marine Insurance">519 F.3d 1025</a></span><pre class="inline">, 1031 
23  (9th Cir. 2008).                                                          
24                            III.  DISCUSSION                                
25    A. Extrinsic Evidence                                                   
26       Defendants cite extrinsic evidence in support of their Opposition.  (See Doc. 60.) 
27  Plaintiffs cite extrinsic evidence in support of their Reply.  (See Doc. 63.)  However, 
28  Plaintiffs object to the Court considering Defendants’ extrinsic evidence because Plaintiffs 
1  must be given notice and an opportunity to present all their evidence prior to the Motion 
2  being converted into a motion for summary judgment.  (See </pre><span class="citation" data-id="1194077"><a href="/opinion/1194077/manzarek-v-st-paul-fire-marine-insurance/" aria-description="Citation for case: Manzarek v. St. Paul Fire &amp; Marine Insurance">id.</a></span><pre class="inline"> at 4–5.)    
3       The Court declines to exercise its discretion to convert Plaintiffs’ Motion into a 
4  motion for summary judgment.  See Yakima Valley Mem’l Hosp., </pre><span class="citation" data-id="223637"><a href="/opinion/223637/yakima-valley-memorial-hospital-v-washington-state-department-of-health/" aria-description="Citation for case: Yakima Valley Memorial Hospital v. Washington State...">654 F.3d at 925</a></span><pre class="inline"> n.6 (“The 
5  district court had discretion not to convert the motion for judgment on the pleadings into a 
6  summary judgment motion.”).  The Court is not persuaded Plaintiffs’ Reply presents a 
7  sufficient opportunity for Plaintiffs to present evidence relevant to a motion for summary 
8  judgment under Rule 12(d).3  See Open Text Corp. v. Hyland Software, Inc., Case Nos. 
9  8:20-cv-2116 DOC (ADSx), 8:20-cv-2123 DOC (ADSx), </pre><span class="citation no-link">2021 WL 3558788</span><pre class="inline">, at *2 (C.D. 
10  Cal. May 28, 2021) (“It is reversible error for a court to grant a motion to dismiss that has 
11  been converted to one for summary judgment, without providing all parties a reasonable 
12  opportunity to present material relevant to a Rule 56 motion.”) (citations omitted).   
13       Thus,  the  Court  will  not  consider  extrinsic  evidence  submitted  by  the  parties.  
14  Similarly, the Court will not address arguments solely relying on such extrinsic evidence. 
15    B. Judicial Notice

21  (1) “generally known within the trial court’s territorial jurisdiction; or (2) can be accurately 
22  and readily determined from sources whose accuracy cannot reasonably be questioned.” 
23  Fed. R. Evid. 201(b).  “Because judicial notice ‘bypasses the safeguards which are involved

18  Motion as applying to the SACC.  See Shame on You Prods., Inc. v. Elizabeth Banks, 120

24    D. Timeliness                                                           
25       Defendants argue Plaintiffs’ Motion is untimely and will delay trial.  (Doc. 60 at 14–

27  pleadings close, discovery is complete, and note that a trial date has not been set in this 
28  case.  (Doc. 63 at 2.)                                                    
1       Rule 12(c) allows parties to move for judgment on the pleadings after the pleadings 
2  have been closed but prior to trial, and “within such time as not to delay the trial.”  Fed. R. 
3  Civ. P. 12(c).  Plaintiffs filed their Motion on the final day for pretrial motions pursuant to 
4  the scheduling order in this case.  (See Docs. 32, 53.)  In light of Plaintiffs’ Motion, the 
5  Court vacated remaining pretrial deadlines on July 20, 2023.  (Doc. 68.)  While discovery 
6  has closed, a trial date has not been set.  See Jaeger v. Howmedica Osteonics Corp., Case 
7  No. 15-cv-00164-HSG, </pre><span class="citation no-link">2016 WL 520985</span><pre class="inline">, at *6 (N.D. Cal. Feb. 10, 2016) (finding motion 
8  for judgment on the pleadings not untimely where trial date was over seven months away); 
9  Perez v. Wells Fargo &amp; Co., </pre><span class="citation" data-id="7229065"><a href="/opinion/7311161/perez-v-wells-fargo-co/#1190" aria-description="Citation for case: Perez v. Wells Fargo &amp; Co.">75 F. Supp. 3d 1184, 1190</a></span><pre class="inline"> (N.D. Cal. 2014) (finding 12(c) 
10  motion not untimely where trial date was not set and the idea that the motion cannot lie

13  v. Maggio, No. C05-5766RJB, </pre><span class="citation no-link">2007 WL 2900180</span><pre class="inline">, at *6 (W.D. Wash. Oct. 1, 2007) is 
14  misplaced.  Even if the Maggio court did construe the defendants’ Rule 9(b) motion as a

16  close to the trial date.  See </pre><span class="citation no-link">id.</span><pre class="inline">  Conversely here, no trial date has been set.  
17       Thus, Plaintiffs’ Motion is timely.                                  
18    E. Standing of Defendant Melone                                         
19       Plaintiffs contend that Defendant Melone lacks standing to assert each cause of 
20  action.  (Doc. 56-1 at 13.)  Plaintiffs explain these contentions further for the trade libel, 
21  intentional and negligent interference with prospective economic advantage, and unfair 
22  competition law claims, but not the declaratory relief claim.  (See </pre><span class="citation no-link">id.</span><pre class="inline"> at 14–15, 26, 29–30.)  
23  The  Court  will  first  consider  Defendant  Melone’s  standing  for  the  trade  libel  and 
24  intentional and negligent interference with prospective economic advantage claims.  Later, 
25  the Court will consider Defendants’ standing for the unfair competition law claim.  The 
26  Court does not reach the standing issue on the declaratory relief claim.  
27         a.  Trade Libel                                                    
28       Plaintiffs argue Defendant Melone does not have standing to bring a trade libel claim 
1  because trade libel concerns false statements about one’s business or products.  (Doc. 56-
2  1 at 14–15.)  Defendants respond that Plaintiffs’ libel specifically targeted Defendant 
3  Melone, and he has a direct personal interest in APM affording him standing to pursue a 
4  trade libel claim.  (Doc. 60 at 24–25.)  Plaintiffs respond that Defendant Melone, as an 
5  individual, is not a business or product that can assert a trade libel claim and no alleged 
6  false statements in the SACC refer to Melone directly.  (Doc. 63 at 6–7.) 
7       Plaintiffs have pointed to no case where an owner of a business did not have standing 
8  to bring a trade libel claim concerning the quality of the goods they sell.  (See Doc. 56-1 at 
9  14–15.)  A review of case law in this area reveals that most trade libel claims are brought 
10  by businesses.  However, the Court has identified several cases where owners, or both 
11  owners and their businesses, brought trade libel claims.  See Kovesdy v. Kovesdy, No. C 
12  10-02012 SBA, </pre><span class="citation no-link">2010 WL 3619826</span><pre class="inline">, at *4 (N.D. Cal. Sept. 13, 2010) (finding plaintiff, a 
13  sole proprietor, adequately pled a claim for trade libel for false statements directed at the 
14  character of his business i.e., his competence to provide tax preparation services); Allen v. 
15  Ghoulish Gallery, Civil No. 06cv371 NLS, </pre><span class="citation no-link">2007 WL 4207923</span><pre class="inline">, at *15–16 (S.D. Cal. Nov. 
16  20, 2007) (denying business owner’s trade libel claim because the alleged statements about 
17  the quality of his goods were not sufficiently factual to be proven right or wrong); Franklin 
18  v. Dynamic Details, Inc., </pre><span class="citation" data-id="2276671"><a href="/opinion/2276671/franklin-v-dynamic-details-inc/" aria-description="Citation for case: Franklin v. Dynamic Details, Inc.">116 Cal. App. 4th 375</a></span><pre class="inline">, 383–90 (2004) (denying owner and his 
19  company’s trade libel claim because the emails at issue were opinions, vague, or true 
20  statements); Erlich v. Etner, </pre><span class="citation" data-id="2197589"><a href="/opinion/2197589/erlich-v-etner/" aria-description="Citation for case: Erlich v. Etner">224 Cal. App. 2d 69</a></span><pre class="inline">, 73–76 (1964) (holding owner’s trade 
21  libel claim failed due to not alleging special damages).  Thus, Defendant Melone has 
22  standing to sue for trade libel concerning false statements about Defendant APM.   
23         b.  Intentional and Negligent Interference with Economic Advantage 
24       Plaintiffs argue Defendant Melone cannot assert an intentional interference with 
25  prospective  economic  advantage  claim  because  there  are  no  facts  pled  that  show  a 
26  prospective advantage to Defendant Melone and that the claim solely concerns prospective 
27  economic relations between Plaintiff APM and its potential customers.  (Doc. 56-1 at 26.)  
28  Plaintiffs point to no statute or case law to support their position.  (See id.)  Plaintiffs 
1  reassert all arguments concerning the intentional interference with economic advantage 
2  claim for the negligent interference with economic advantage claim.  (Doc. 56-1 at 29.) 
3       Defendants, including Defendant Melone, allege that they “were in an economic 
4  relationship with potential and actual customers of its pizza business that would have 
5  resulted in an economic benefit” to them and that Plaintiffs disrupted their relationship with 
6  some customers.  (SACC ¶¶ 42, 45.)  This is sufficient for standing purposes for the 
7  common law claims of intentional and negligent interference with prospective economic 
8  advantage.  See Sacramento E.D.M., Inc. v. Hynes Aviation Indus., Inc., </pre><span class="citation" data-id="2660122"><a href="/opinion/2660122/mcnamara-v-picken/#1141" aria-description="Citation for case: McNamara v. Picken">965 F. Supp. 2d 
9  1141, 1146, 1153</a></span><pre class="inline"> (E.D. Cal. 2013) (denying motion to dismiss intentional and negligent 
10  interference  with  prospective  economic  advantage  claims  brought  by  company  and 
11  company’s president); c.f. Davis v. Maryland Bank, No. 00-04191, </pre><span class="citation no-link">2002 WL 32713429</span><pre class="inline">, at 
12  *7 (N.D. Cal. June 19, 2002) (finding a company did not have standing for an intentional 
13  interference with prospective economic advantage claim because the complaint alleged the 
14  individual plaintiff was the one with prospective contracts that were terminated as a result 
15  of the defendant’s willful conduct, not the company); Billmeyer v. Plaza Bank of Com., 42 
</pre><span class="citation" data-id="2253276"><a href="/opinion/2253276/people-v-muniz/" aria-description="Citation for case: People v. Muniz">16  Cal. App. 4th 1086</a></span><pre class="inline">, 1098–99 (1995) (finding individual plaintiffs lacked standing for 
17  intentional interference with economic relationship claim where the complaint alleged the 
18  bankruptcy estate, not the individual plaintiffs, suffered damages from the interference).  
19  Thus, Defendant Melone has standing to sue for intentional and negligent interference with 
20  prospective economic advantage.                                           
21    F. Trade Libel (First Cause of Action)                                  
22       “Trade libel is defined as ‘an intentional disparagement of the quality of property, 
23  which results in pecuniary damage....’”  Aetna Cas. &amp; Sur. Co. v. Centennial Ins. Co., 838

Etner">224 Cal. App. 2d at 73</a></span><pre class="inline">).  “Unlike classic 
25  defamation, they are not directed at the plaintiff’s personal reputation but rather at the

28       “To prove trade libel, Plaintiff must show (1) a statement that (2) was false, (3)

1    H. Negligent Interference with Prospective Economic Relations (Third Cause of

3       Plaintiffs argue the negligent interference with prospective economic relations claim

8       “[A] plaintiff bringing a claim for negligent interference with prospective economic 
9  advantage must sufficiently allege: (1) an economic relationship existed between the 
10  plaintiff and a third party which contained a reasonably probable future economic benefit 
11  or advantage to plaintiff; (2) the defendant knew of the existence of the relationship and 
12  was aware or should have been aware that if it did not act with due care its actions would 
13  interfere with this relationship and cause plaintiff to lose in whole or in part the probable 
14  future economic benefit or advantage of the relationship; (3) the defendant was negligent; 
15  and (4) such negligence cause[d] damage to plaintiff in that the relationship was actually 
16  interfered with or disrupted and plaintiff lost in whole or in part the economic benefits or 
17  advantage reasonably expected from the relationship.”  F.M. Tarbell Co. v. A &amp; L Partners, 
18  Inc., No. CV 10-1589 PSG (Ex), </pre><span class="citation no-link">2011 WL 1153539</span><pre class="inline">, at *5 (C.D. Cal. Mar. 23, 2011) (citing

Superior Court of Los...">59 Cal. App. 4th 764, 786</a></span><pre class="inline"> (1997)).

21  campaign of unlawful harassment” against Defendants and they “knew or should have
