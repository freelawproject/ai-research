<opinion type="majority">
<p id="b446-21">
  MEMORANDUM OF DECISION AND ORDER
 </p>
<author id="b446-22">
  JOHN F. RAY, Jr., Bankruptcy Judge.
 </author>
<p id="b446-23">
  This matter came before the Court on the objection of creditors, Marion Burns and Avery S. Friedman, to confirmation of debtor’s proposed plan filed under Chapter 13 of the Bankruptcy Code.
 </p>
<p id="b446-24">
  Debtor, Guiseppi Celeste, proposes to pay all real estate mortgages in full outside the plan, and to pay into the plan $200.00 per month for the benefit of unsecured creditors. If debtor completes the plan, unsecured creditors would receive about eight percent.
 </p>
<p id="b447-3">
<span citation-index="1" class="star-pagination" label="393"> 
   *393
   </span>
  By agreement of counsel, the matter was submitted to the Court on the pleadings and briefs, no evidentiary hearing being held.
 </p>
<p id="b447-4">
  The creditors objected to debtor’s plan on two grounds:
 </p>
<p id="b447-5">
  (A) The proposed eight percent payment to unsecured creditors, is, as a matter of law, too small to permit confirmation of the plan under Section 1324 of the Code; and
 </p>
<p id="b447-6">
  (B) That creditors’ debt is nondischargeable under Section 523(a)(6), because it is a judgment awarding compensatory and punitive damages in a racial discrimination case.
 </p>
<p id="b447-7">
  Creditors’ first objection is not supported by the Code. Section 1325(a)(4), the only section proscribing a quantitative minimum for Chapter 13, requires that creditors receive as much in Chapter 13 as they would have received in Chapter 7.
  <a class="footnote" href="#fn1" id="fn1_ref">
   1
  </a>
  It is undisputed in this case that this minimum standard has been met.
 </p>
<p id="b447-8">
  The only basis for objecting to debt- or’s plan is that it does not meet the good faith standard required by Section 1325(a)(3).
  <a class="footnote" href="#fn2" id="fn2_ref">
   2
  </a>
  This Court is well aware of the extent and variety of opinions written on the good faith requirement. Bankruptcy decisions have ranged from confirmation of zero percent plans to requiring 70 percent plans.
  <a class="footnote" href="#fn3" id="fn3_ref">
   3
  </a>
  Many courts have chosen to apply a flexible standard wherein factors, such as the debtor’s future income prospects, present ability to pay, proposed payout to unsecured debts, and the use of the broader discharge of Chapter 13, are considered in determining whether good faith exists.
  <a class="footnote" href="#fn4" id="fn4_ref">
   4
  </a>
  The flexible standard, however, has not led to uniformity of result.
 </p>
<p id="b447-14">
  In interpreting a statute such as this, the courts should be primarily guided by the legislative intent as expressed by the Code.
  <a class="footnote" href="#fn5" id="fn5_ref">
   5
  </a>
  Any substantial revision of Chapter 13 must be left to Congress.
 </p>
<p id="b447-15">
  An analysis of the Code reveals that Congress had the opportunity to establish a minimum payout for Chapter 13, if it had so desired. Section 727(a)(9) of the Code does not allow a debtor to be discharged in a subsequent Chapter 7, unless the debtor’s prior Chapter 13 paid at least 70 percent to unsecured creditors, was filed in good faith and represented the debtor’s best efforts. If debtor’s prior Chapter 13 plan paid 100 percent to unsecureds, then the plan did not have to be filed in good faith or represent the debtor’s best effort.
  <a class="footnote" href="#fn6" id="fn6_ref">
   6
  </a>
  With such guide lines established by Section 727(a), it is difficult to believe that Congress had mini
  <span citation-index="1" class="star-pagination" label="394"> 
   *394
   </span>
  mum payment percentages intended in Chapter 13 when it drafted the term “good faith.” Therefore, this Court, in the present case, cannot assume as a matter of law that an eight percent payment to unsecured creditors lacks good faith.
 </p>
<p id="b448-4">
  Creditors’ second objection to debt- or’s plan is also not well taken. It is quite clear that creditors’ debt does not fall within the Chapter 13 exceptions to discharge.
  <a class="footnote" href="#fn7" id="fn7_ref">
   7
  </a>
  These creditors necessarily base their objection on the good faith standard of Section 1325(a)(3), and courts have varied in their decisions on this issue.
  <a class="footnote" href="#fn8" id="fn8_ref">
   8
  </a>
</p>
<p id="b448-5">
  This Court concludes that the inclusion of an alleged Chapter 7 nondischargeable debt in an eight percent Chapter 13 plan does not, as a matter of law, preclude confirmation. The plan, as proposed by debtor, conforms to all the requirements of Section 1325 and, therefore, should be confirmed.
 </p>
<p id="b448-6">
  On the basis of the foregoing memorandum of decision, which is hereby adopted as my findings of fact and conclusions of law pursuant to Bankruptcy Rule 752, it is
 </p>
<p id="b448-7">
  ORDERED that the Chapter 13 plan proposed by debtor, Guiseppe Celeste, be, and the same is hereby confirmed.
 </p>
