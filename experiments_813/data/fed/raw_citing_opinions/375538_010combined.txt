<opinion type="majority">
<author id="b909-10">
  FRANK M. JOHNSON, Jr., Circuit Judge:
 </author>
<p id="b909-11">
  Milton Harvey Brown, III, was indicted and convicted in the United States District Court for the Northern District of Florida as an aider and abettor in the robbery of the Gulf Breeze Branch of the First Federal Savings and Loan Association of Santa Rosa County, Florida (First Federal).
  <a class="footnote" href="#fn1" id="fn1_ref">
   1
  </a>
  Brown appeals his conviction. We affirm.
 </p>
<p id="b909-18">
  Brown mounts a two-pronged attack on his conviction. At issue is the court’s instruction to the jury on the question of the status of First Federal as an institution within the meaning of <span class="citation no-link">18 U.S.C. § 2113</span>, the federal bank robbery statute,
  <a class="footnote" href="#fn2" id="fn2_ref">
   2
  </a>
  and the sufficiency of the evidence introduced to demonstrate the requisite status.
 </p>
<p id="b909-19">
  I. Jury Instruction
 </p>
<p id="b909-20">
  In <span class="citation no-link">18 U.S.C. § 2113</span>(g)
  <a class="footnote" href="#fn3" id="fn3_ref">
   3
  </a>
  Congress provided for three “alternative bases” for federal jurisdiction over robbery of a savings and loan association.
  <em>
   United States v. Fitzpatrick,
  </em>
  <span class="citation" data-id="358826"><a href="/opinion/358826/united-states-v-robert-arthur-fitzpatrick/#1222" aria-description="Citation for case: United States v. Robert Arthur Fitzpatrick">581 F.2d 1221, 1222-23</a></span> (5th Cir. 1978) (per curiam). Section 2113 governs such crimes if the savings and loan is “either a federally chartered institution, a federally insured institution, or a federal credit union . .”
  <em>
   United States v. Fitzpatrick, supra,
  </em>
  <span class="citation" data-id="358826"><a href="/opinion/358826/united-states-v-robert-arthur-fitzpatrick/#1223" aria-description="Citation for case: United States v. Robert Arthur Fitzpatrick">581 F.2d at 1223</a></span>. Because the indictment against Brown charged that First Federal’s deposits were insured by the Federal Savings and Loan Insurance Corporation (FSLIC) at the time of the robbery,
  <em>
   see
  </em>
  note 1
  <em>
   supra,
  </em>
  “proof of the insured status of [First Federal] was an essential element of the crime charged, and indeed had to be proved in order to establish federal jurisdiction.
  <em>
   See United States v. Murrah,
  </em>
  478
  <span citation-index="1" class="star-pagination" label="846"> 
   *846
   </span>
  F.2d 762, 764 (5th Cir. 1973).”
  <em>
   United States v. Fitzpatrick, supra,
  </em>
  <span class="citation" data-id="358826"><a href="/opinion/358826/united-states-v-robert-arthur-fitzpatrick/#1223" aria-description="Citation for case: United States v. Robert Arthur Fitzpatrick">581 F.2d at 1223</a></span>.
 </p>
<p id="b910-4">
  The district court in its instructions to the jury read the entire indictment and the pertinent portions of Section 2113(a).
  <em>
   See
  </em>
  note 2
  <em>
   supra.
  </em>
  Immediately thereafter, the court charged the jury that:
 </p>
<blockquote id="b910-5">
  In order to establish the offense of bank robbery as charged in the indictment there are three essential elements that must be proved beyond a reasonable doubt: First, the act or acts of taking from the person or presence of another any money belonging to or in the possession of a bank or savings and loan association
  <em>
   as
  </em>
  charged; secondly, the act or acts of taking such money by force or violence or by means of intimidation; and, third, that such acts were done willfully.
 </blockquote>
<blockquote id="b910-6">
  As used in this law a bank means any bank the deposits of which are insured by the Federal Deposit Insurance Corporation. And a savings and loan association means any federal savings and loan association. (Emphasis added.)
 </blockquote>
<p id="b910-7">
  The court committed error in its failure specifically to instruct the jury with respect to the jurisdictional basis — FSLIC insurance — charged in the indictment and asserted at trial.
  <em>
   See United States v. Fitzpatrick, supra,
  </em>
  <span class="citation" data-id="358826"><a href="/opinion/358826/united-states-v-robert-arthur-fitzpatrick/#1223" aria-description="Citation for case: United States v. Robert Arthur Fitzpatrick">581 F.2d at 1223</a></span>;
  <em>
   United States v. Bosch,
  </em>
  <span class="citation" data-id="322500"><a href="/opinion/322500/united-states-v-nemia-bosch/#82" aria-description="Citation for case: United States v. Nemia Bosch">505 F.2d 78, 82</a></span> (5th Cir. 1974). Despite the requirements of Rule 30, Fed.R.Crim.P.,
  <a class="footnote" href="#fn4" id="fn4_ref">
   4
  </a>
  however, at trial Brown raised no objection to the charge. Because of Brown’s failure to object at trial we will not reverse unless the error constitutes “plain error[ ] . . . affecting substantial rights” that “may be noticed [on appeal] although . . not brought to the attention of the [district] court.” Fed.R.Crim.P. 52(b).
  <a class="footnote" href="#fn5" id="fn5_ref">
   5
  </a>
</p>
<p id="b910-14">
  Dicta to the contrary notwithstanding,
  <em>
   see United States v. White,
  </em>
  <span class="citation" data-id="372808"><a href="/opinion/372808/united-states-v-charles-robert-white/#536" aria-description="Citation for case: United States v. Charles Robert White">611 F.2d 531, 536</a></span> (5th Cir. 1980);
  <em>
   United States v. Musgrave,
  </em>
  <span class="citation" data-id="297534"><a href="/opinion/297534/united-states-v-kenneth-l-musgrave-and-jack-bryant/#764" aria-description="Citation for case: United States v. Kenneth L. Musgrave and Jack Bryant">444 F.2d 755, 764</a></span> (5th Cir. 1971),
  <em>
   cert. denied,
  </em>
  <span class="citation multiple-matches"><a href="/c/U.S./414/1023/">414 U.S. 1023</a></span>, <span class="citation multiple-matches"><a href="/c/S.Ct./94/447/">94 S.Ct. 447</a></span>, <span class="citation multiple-matches"><a href="/c/L.Ed.2d/38/315/">38 L.Ed.2d 315</a></span> (1973), we divine from precedent in this Circuit no rule that failure specifically to instruct on any single essential element of a crime per se constitutes plain error.
  <em>
   See United States v. White, supra,
  </em>
  <span class="citation" data-id="372808"><a href="/opinion/372808/united-states-v-charles-robert-white/#537" aria-description="Citation for case: United States v. Charles Robert White">611 F.2d at 537</a></span>;
  <em>
   United States v. Fitzpatrick, supra,
  </em>
  <span class="citation" data-id="358826"><a href="/opinion/358826/united-states-v-robert-arthur-fitzpatrick/#1222" aria-description="Citation for case: United States v. Robert Arthur Fitzpatrick">581 F.2d at 1222</a></span> (“failure to instruct ... on
  <em>
   this
  </em>
  essential element”);
  <em>
   United States v. Beasley,
  </em>
  <span class="citation" data-id="328770"><a href="/opinion/328770/united-states-v-noble-c-beasley/#244" aria-description="Citation for case: United States v. Noble C. Beasley">519 F.2d 233, 244</a></span> (5th Cir. 1975),
  <em>
   vacated on other grounds,
  </em>
  <span class="citation multiple-matches"><a href="/c/U.S./425/956/">425 U.S. 956</a></span>, <span class="citation multiple-matches"><a href="/c/S.Ct./96/1736/">96 S.Ct. 1736</a></span>, <span class="citation no-link">48 L.Ed.2d 201</span> (1976);
  <em>
   United States v. Bosch, supra,
  </em>
  <span class="citation" data-id="322500"><a href="/opinion/322500/united-states-v-nemia-bosch/#82" aria-description="Citation for case: United States v. Nemia Bosch">505 F.2d at 82</a></span> (jury verdict “limited by judicially fashioned blinders to a single specified fact issue”);
  <em>
   United States v. Ragano,
  </em>
  <span class="citation" data-id="9459342"><a href="/opinion/310127/united-states-v-frank-ragano/#417" aria-description="Citation for case: United States v. Frank Ragano">476 F.2d 410, 417-18</a></span> (5th Cir. 1973);
  <em>
   United States v. Harper,
  </em>
  <span class="citation" data-id="303567"><a href="/opinion/303567/united-states-v-wesley-leon-harper/#707" aria-description="Citation for case: United States v. Wesley Leon Harper">460 F.2d 705, 707</a></span> (5th Cir. 1972);
  <em>
   United States v. Musgrave, supra,
  </em>
  <span class="citation" data-id="297534"><a href="/opinion/297534/united-states-v-kenneth-l-musgrave-and-jack-bryant/#763" aria-description="Citation for case: United States v. Kenneth L. Musgrave and Jack Bryant">444 F.2d at 763-64</a></span> (omission of two separate essential elements);
  <em>
   United States v. Urbana,
  </em>
  <span class="citation" data-id="285519"><a href="/opinion/285519/united-states-v-sam-frank-urbana/#1083" aria-description="Citation for case: United States v. Sam Frank Urbana">412 F.2d 1081, 1083</a></span> (5th Cir. 1969),
  <em>
   cert. denied,
  </em>
  <span class="citation multiple-matches"><a href="/c/U.S./400/821/">400 U.S. 821</a></span>, <span class="citation multiple-matches"><a href="/c/S.Ct./91/39/">91 S.Ct. 39</a></span>, <span class="citation" data-id="8972476"><a href="/opinion/8980600/detroit-vital-foods-inc-v-united-states/" aria-description="Citation for case: Detroit Vital Foods, Inc. v. United States">27 L.Ed.2d 49</a></span> (1970) (failure to instruct on all essential elements might have been harmless). Moreover, other circuits also have eschewed a per se analysis.
  <em>
   United States v. Singleton,
  </em>
  <span class="citation" data-id="334130"><a href="/opinion/334130/united-states-v-gary-singleton/#205" aria-description="Citation for case: United States v. Gary Singleton">532 F.2d 199, 205-06</a></span> (2d Cir. 1976);
  <a class="footnote" href="#fn6" id="fn6_ref">
   6
  </a>
<em>
   United
  </em>
<span citation-index="1" class="star-pagination" label="847"> 
   *847
   </span>
<em>
   States v. Natale,
  </em>
  <span class="citation" data-id="8898097"><a href="/opinion/8910410/united-states-v-natale/#1167" aria-description="Citation for case: United States v. Natale">526 F.2d 1160, 1167</a></span> (2d Cir. 1975),
  <em>
   cert. denied,
  </em>
  <span class="citation multiple-matches"><a href="/c/U.S./425/950/">425 U.S. 950</a></span>, <span class="citation multiple-matches"><a href="/c/S.Ct./96/1724/">96 S.Ct. 1724</a></span>, <span class="citation no-link">48 L.Ed.2d 193</span> (1976);
  <em>
   United States
  </em>
  v.
  <em>
   Bryant,
  </em>
  <span class="citation" data-id="303950"><a href="/opinion/303950/united-states-v-burnis-bryant-united-states-of-america-v-henry-church/#921" aria-description="Citation for case: United States v. Burnis Bryant, United States of America...">461 F.2d 912, 921</a></span> (6th Cir. 1972);
  <em>
   see United States v. Williams,
  </em>
  <span class="citation" data-id="304603"><a href="/opinion/304603/united-states-v-louis-l-williams/#962" aria-description="Citation for case: United States v. Louis L. Williams">463 F.2d 958, 962</a></span> (D.C.Cir.1972) (failure to instruct on any of the elements of the felony underlying conviction of felony murder);
  <em>
   Byrd v. United States,
  </em>
  <span class="citation" data-id="267235"><a href="/opinion/267235/arthur-eugene-byrd-v-united-states/#940" aria-description="Citation for case: Arthur Eugene Byrd v. United States">342 F.2d 939, 940</a></span> (D.C.Cir.1965) (neither recitation of elements nor a discussion of those elements).
  <a class="footnote" href="#fn7" id="fn7_ref">
   7
  </a>
<em>
   But see United States v. King,
  </em>
  <span class="citation" data-id="9465290"><a href="/opinion/361133/united-states-v-david-s-king-united-states-of-america-v-stanley-e/#965" aria-description="Citation for case: United States v. David S. King, United States of America...">587 F.2d 956, 965-66</a></span> (9th Cir. 1978) (failure to charge an essential element is “generally” plain error).
 </p>
<p id="b911-6">
  Absent operation of a per se rule we examine the circumstances of the present ease to determine whether the court’s error in failing specifically to enumerate the jurisdictional element of federal insurance constitutes plain error affecting Brown’s “substantial rights.” Brown urges that we are bound by our decision in
  <em>
   United States v. <span class="citation" data-id="358826"><a href="/opinion/358826/united-states-v-robert-arthur-fitzpatrick/" aria-description="Citation for case: United States v. Robert Arthur Fitzpatrick">Fitzpatrick, supra,</a></span>
  </em>
  to hold the court’s error here reversible. We disagree. The facts of
  <em>
   <span class="citation" data-id="358826"><a href="/opinion/358826/united-states-v-robert-arthur-fitzpatrick/" aria-description="Citation for case: United States v. Robert Arthur Fitzpatrick">Fitzpatrick</a></span>
  </em>
  are clearly distinguishable from those involved in the present case.
 </p>
<p id="b911-7">
  In
  <em>
   <span class="citation" data-id="358826"><a href="/opinion/358826/united-states-v-robert-arthur-fitzpatrick/" aria-description="Citation for case: United States v. Robert Arthur Fitzpatrick">Fitzpatrick</a></span>
  </em>
  and the present case the indictments based federal jurisdiction upon the fact of FSLIC insurance.
  <em>
   See
  </em>
  <span class="citation" data-id="358826"><a href="/opinion/358826/united-states-v-robert-arthur-fitzpatrick/#1222" aria-description="Citation for case: United States v. Robert Arthur Fitzpatrick">581 F.2d at 1222</a></span>; note 1
  <em>
   supra.
  </em>
  Similarly, in both cases the government sought to prove that the institutions were federally insured.
  <em>
   See
  </em>
  <span class="citation" data-id="358826"><a href="/opinion/358826/united-states-v-robert-arthur-fitzpatrick/#1223" aria-description="Citation for case: United States v. Robert Arthur Fitzpatrick">581 F.2d at 1223</a></span>. In
  <em>
   <span class="citation" data-id="358826"><a href="/opinion/358826/united-states-v-robert-arthur-fitzpatrick/" aria-description="Citation for case: United States v. Robert Arthur Fitzpatrick">Fitzpatrick</a></span>,
  </em>
  the court charged the jury that jurisdiction depended upon proof of a federal charter, an alternative basis for federal jurisdiction.
  <em>
   Id.; see
  </em>
  note 3
  <em>
   supra
  </em>
  and accompanying text. The
  <em>
   <span class="citation" data-id="358826"><a href="/opinion/358826/united-states-v-robert-arthur-fitzpatrick/" aria-description="Citation for case: United States v. Robert Arthur Fitzpatrick">Fitzpatrick</a></span>
  </em>
  court found plain error and reversed because:
 </p>
<blockquote id="b911-8">
  The district court’s instruction on the presence of a federal charter — an alternative statutory basis for federal jurisdiction which the government never charged and never sought to prove — cannot serve in place of a proper instruction on federally insured status, the basis charged in the indictment and asserted at trial.
 </blockquote>
<p id="b911-12">
  <span class="citation" data-id="358826"><a href="/opinion/358826/united-states-v-robert-arthur-fitzpatrick/#1223" aria-description="Citation for case: United States v. Robert Arthur Fitzpatrick">581 F.2d at 1223</a></span>.
 </p>
<p id="b911-13">
  If the instruction on jurisdiction in the present case contained no language beyond the statement that “a ‘savings and loan association’ means any federal savings and loan association,” we would agree with Brown’s position that
  <em>
   <span class="citation" data-id="358826"><a href="/opinion/358826/united-states-v-robert-arthur-fitzpatrick/" aria-description="Citation for case: United States v. Robert Arthur Fitzpatrick">Fitzpatrick</a></span>
  </em>
  controls here. The language quoted above, however, is not the only reference to jurisdiction made in the court’s jury charge at Brown’s trial. In its charge to the jury the court enumerated three elements of the crime with which Brown was charged. In that enumeration the judge stated the requirement that the jury find that the robbery was of “a savings and loan institution
  <em>
   as charged.”
  </em>
  (Emphasis added.) Nothing similar occurred in
  <em>
   <span class="citation" data-id="358826"><a href="/opinion/358826/united-states-v-robert-arthur-fitzpatrick/" aria-description="Citation for case: United States v. Robert Arthur Fitzpatrick">Fitzpatrick</a></span>;
  </em>
  we believe the factual distinction is critical.
 </p>
<p id="b911-14">
  Clearly, the court cannot avoid completely its duty to instruct on the elements of the offense by merely incorporating or referring to the indictment.
  <em>
   See United States v. Bosch, supra,
  </em>
  <span class="citation" data-id="322500"><a href="/opinion/322500/united-states-v-nemia-bosch/#82" aria-description="Citation for case: United States v. Nemia Bosch">505 F.2d at 82</a></span>;
  <em>
   United States v. Williams, supra,
  </em>
  <span class="citation" data-id="304603"><a href="/opinion/304603/united-states-v-louis-l-williams/#960" aria-description="Citation for case: United States v. Louis L. Williams">463 F.2d at 960-62</a></span>;
  <em>
   Lott v. United States,
  </em>
  <span class="citation" data-id="235587"><a href="/opinion/235587/john-thomas-lott-johnnie-lee-reed-larry-pearce-joe-melvin-shaw-and-doris/" aria-description="Citation for case: John Thomas Lott, Johnnie Lee Reed, Larry Pearce, Joe...">218 F.2d 675</a></span>, 680-81 n. 7 (5th Cir. 1955). Nevertheless, in deciding that a failure to instruct a jury on an element of the crime was
  <em>
   not
  </em>
  plain error, this Court has recognized the ameliorative effects of reading or referring to the indictment.
  <em>
   United States v. White, supra,
  </em>
  <span class="citation" data-id="372808"><a href="/opinion/372808/united-states-v-charles-robert-white/#537" aria-description="Citation for case: United States v. Charles Robert White">611 F.2d at 537</a></span>;
  <em>
   United States v. Beasley, supra,
  </em>
  <span class="citation" data-id="328770"><a href="/opinion/328770/united-states-v-noble-c-beasley/#245" aria-description="Citation for case: United States v. Noble C. Beasley">519 F.2d at 245</a></span>. In
  <em>
   <span class="citation" data-id="372808"><a href="/opinion/372808/united-states-v-charles-robert-white/" aria-description="Citation for case: United States v. Charles Robert White">White</a></span>,
  </em>
  the court read the indictment to the jury before the jury was sworn, instructed the jury that the offense of “forging . as charged” must be proved beyond a reasonable doubt, and sent the indictment with the jury into the jury room for considera
  <span citation-index="1" class="star-pagination" label="848"> 
   *848
   </span>
  tion. <span class="citation" data-id="372808"><a href="/opinion/372808/united-states-v-charles-robert-white/#537" aria-description="Citation for case: United States v. Charles Robert White">611 F.2d at 537</a></span>. Those, facts are all present here. Additionally, however, the district court in the present case read the indictment at the beginning of its charge to the jury. Moreover, the indictment is neither long nor complex; in one count and one sentence it charged Brown with aiding and abetting in the robbery of First Federal. Finally, in the court’s charge to the jury the portion of the indictment specifying that First Federal was federally insured precedes by only ten transcript lines the instruction that the robber must have taken “money belonging to or in the possession of a . savings and loan association as
  <em>
   charged.”
  </em>
  (Emphasis added.) Under the circumstances, we believe the court “adequately informed the jury of the requisites to a finding of guilty . . . .”
  <em>
   United States v. Beasley, supra,
  </em>
  <span class="citation" data-id="328770"><a href="/opinion/328770/united-states-v-noble-c-beasley/#245" aria-description="Citation for case: United States v. Noble C. Beasley">519 F.2d at 245</a></span>. The trial court’s error in instructing the jury was not plain error requiring reversal.
 </p>
<p id="b912-6">
  II. Sufficiency of the Evidence
 </p>
<p id="b912-7">
  Brown also urges reversal of his conviction because of claimed insufficiency of the government’s proof that First Federal was a federally insured institution. The contention, however, is not persuasive. The court did not err in denying Brown’s motion for judgment of acquittal.
 </p>
<p id="b912-8">
  The government’s evidence of the insured status of First Federal consisted of a 1961 telegram and the testimony of Mr. Warren D. Jarvis, managing officer of First Federal. The telegram, sent by the comptroller of the FSLIC notified First Federal that FLSIC insurance became effective on April 25, 1961, under certificate of insurance number 4498. Mr. Jarvis testified that First Federal was insured at the time of the robbery. Although he did not become associated with the institution until several months after the robbery,
  <a class="footnote" href="#fn8" id="fn8_ref">
   8
  </a>
  Mr. Jarvis as custodian of the business records knew that the insurance premiums had been paid. Moreover, Mr. Jarvis testified that in August, 1978, he personally examined FSLIC certificate of insurance number 4498.
  <a class="footnote" href="#fn9" id="fn9_ref">
   9
  </a>
</p>
<p id="b912-11">
  As in a number of cases decided by this Court, the government’s proof of federal insurance was sparse. See
  <em>
   United States v. Maner,
  </em>
  <span class="citation" data-id="372585"><a href="/opinion/372585/united-states-v-grady-james-maner/#108" aria-description="Citation for case: United States v. Grady James Maner">611 F.2d 107, 108, 110</a></span> (5th Cir. 1980);
  <em>
   United States v. Williams,
  </em>
  <span class="citation" data-id="363739"><a href="/opinion/363739/united-states-v-nathaniel-lewis-williams-aka-nate-williams-and-lewis/" aria-description="Citation for case: United States v. Nathaniel Lewis Williams, A/K/A Nate...">592 F.2d 1277</a></span> (5th Cir. 1979);
  <em>
   United States v. Fitzpatrick, supra,
  </em>
  <span class="citation" data-id="358826"><a href="/opinion/358826/united-states-v-robert-arthur-fitzpatrick/#1223" aria-description="Citation for case: United States v. Robert Arthur Fitzpatrick">581 F.2d at 1223</a></span>;
  <em>
   United States v. <span class="citation" data-id="310979"><a href="/opinion/310979/united-states-v-joe-grady-murrah/" aria-description="Citation for case: United States v. Joe Grady Murrah">Murrah, supra,</a></span>
  </em>
  <span class="citation" data-id="310979"><a href="/opinion/310979/united-states-v-joe-grady-murrah/#763" aria-description="Citation for case: United States v. Joe Grady Murrah">478 F.2d 762, 763-64</a></span> (5th Cir. 1973);
  <em>
   United States v. Thompson,
  </em>
  <span class="citation" data-id="288276"><a href="/opinion/288276/united-states-v-john-thompson/#379" aria-description="Citation for case: United States v. John Thompson">421 F.2d 373, 379</a></span> (5th Cir.),
  <em>
   vacated on other grounds,
  </em>
  <span class="citation" data-id="108212"><a href="/opinion/108212/thompson-v-united-states/" aria-description="Citation for case: Thompson v. United States">400 U.S. 17</a></span>, <span class="citation multiple-matches"><a href="/c/S.Ct./91/122/">91 S.Ct. 122</a></span>, <span class="citation" data-id="108212"><a href="/opinion/108212/thompson-v-united-states/" aria-description="Citation for case: Thompson v. United States">27 L.Ed.2d 17</a></span> (1970);
  <em>
   Ahlstedt v. United States,
  </em>
  <span class="citation" data-id="262549"><a href="/opinion/262549/john-g-ahlstedt-v-united-states-of-america-john-levay-v-united-states/#260" aria-description="Citation for case: John G. Ahlstedt v. United States of America, John Levay...">325 F.2d 257, 260</a></span> (5th Cir. 1963),
  <em>
   cert. denied,
  </em>
  <span class="citation multiple-matches"><a href="/c/U.S./377/968/">377 U.S. 968</a></span>, <span class="citation no-link">84 S.Ct. 1650</span>, <span class="citation no-link">12 L.Ed.2d 738</span> (1964);
  <em>
   Cook v. United States,
  </em>
  <span class="citation" data-id="261265"><a href="/opinion/261265/don-b-cook-v-united-states/#259" aria-description="Citation for case: Don B. Cook v. United States">320 F.2d 258, 259-60</a></span> (5th Cir. 1963). Sparse evidence, however, can be enough.
 </p>
<p id="b912-12">
  In
  <em>
   United States v. Maner,
  </em>
  this Court exhaustively reviewed the cases considering the issue and “found no case still of precedential value from this Court or any other Circuit Court reversing a . conviction under § 2113 for insufficient proof of insurance.” 611 F.2d at 111;
  <em>
   see id.
  </em>
  at 108-11 &amp; n. 1. Viewing the evidence introduced here in light of case authority carefully examined in
  <em>
   <span class="citation" data-id="372585"><a href="/opinion/372585/united-states-v-grady-james-maner/" aria-description="Citation for case: United States v. Grady James Maner">Maner</a></span>,
  </em>
  we conclude that the government presented sufficient evidence of FSLIC insurance. The telegram and Mr. Jarvis’ statements that FSLIC insurance premiums had been paid and that the certificate of insurance in August, 1978, bore the same number as the certificate issued in 1961 support a jury finding of insurance on the date of the robbery. See
  <em>
   United States v. Maner, supra,
  </em>
  611 F.2d at 110-11;
  <em>
   United States v. Williams, supra,
  </em>
  <span class="citation" data-id="363739"><a href="/opinion/363739/united-states-v-nathaniel-lewis-williams-aka-nate-williams-and-lewis/#1282" aria-description="Citation for case: United States v. Nathaniel Lewis Williams, A/K/A Nate...">592 F.2d at 1282</a></span>;
  <em>
   United States v. Fitzpatrick, supra,
  </em>
  <span class="citation" data-id="358826"><a href="/opinion/358826/united-states-v-robert-arthur-fitzpatrick/#1223" aria-description="Citation for case: United States v. Robert Arthur Fitzpatrick">581 F.2d at 1223</a></span>.
 </p>
<p id="b912-14">
  Although we hold the evidence sufficient to support Brown’s conviction, “the government obviously could have done a much better job . . . .”
  <em>
   United States v.
  </em>
<span citation-index="1" class="star-pagination" label="849"> 
   *849
   </span>
<em>
   Fitzpatrick, supra,
  </em>
  <span class="citation" data-id="358826"><a href="/opinion/358826/united-states-v-robert-arthur-fitzpatrick/#1223" aria-description="Citation for case: United States v. Robert Arthur Fitzpatrick">581 F.2d at 1223</a></span>. “[W]e have difficulty comprehending why the Government repeatedly fails to prove this element more carefully since the Government’s burden is so simple and straightforward. . . . [T]he fault lies not with the Trial Judge. It rests squarely on the shoulders of the prosecutor.”
  <em>
   United States v. Maner, supra,
  </em>
  611 F.2d at 112. In
  <em>
   <span class="citation" data-id="372585"><a href="/opinion/372585/united-states-v-grady-james-maner/" aria-description="Citation for case: United States v. Grady James Maner">Maner</a></span>
  </em>
  the detailed examination of cases considering the problem “reveal[ed] . a nationwide plague infecting United States Attorneys throughout the land. Hopefully the Attorney General will sense and remedy this national deficiency by directions pointing out the simple ways to prove this simple but indispensable fact.”
  <em>
   <span class="citation" data-id="372585"><a href="/opinion/372585/united-states-v-grady-james-maner/" aria-description="Citation for case: United States v. Grady James Maner">Id.</a></span>
  </em>
</p>
<p id="b913-5">
  AFFIRMED.
