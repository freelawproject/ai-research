Taxonomy

All definitions live here: the cases involved in a treatment (citing, cited, acting, target), the three directions a treatment can flow, the four severity tiers, and the full 43-treatment grid (22 base + 21 “as recognized by” variants).

The actors

Every treatment involves at least two opinions: the one applying the treatment and the one receiving it. Two pairs of terms describe the same actors from different angles. We use both pairs in the project — here is what each means and where they differ.

Citing case

the opinion we are reading

The opinion we’re reading — the one that contains the citation. Every reference to an earlier opinion happens inside a citing case.

This is the side that’s fixed: the citing case is whatever opinion the citator is currently processing.

Cited case

any authority referenced by the citing case

An earlier opinion referenced by the citing case. A single citing case typically references many cited cases — every authority it relies on, distinguishes, criticizes, or merely mentions is a cited case.

The citator’s job is to classify the treatment of every cited case inside the citing case.

Acting case

the one applying the treatment

The case that actually performs the treatment. In most situations the acting case is the citing case — the citing case is the one doing the overruling, distinguishing, criticizing, etc.

The exception is the Related Reference direction (below): when the citing case narrates how a third opinion treated the cited case, that third opinion is the acting case — not the citing case.

Target case

the cited case currently being classified

The specific cited case the citator is analyzing at this moment. A citing case has many cited cases, but only one target case at a time — the one whose treatment is being determined.

Every target case is a cited case. “Target” makes the role explicit: it’s the one the classification is aimed at right now.

Direction — how the cases relate and who is acting

A treatment has a direction as well as a label. The direction encodes two things at once: (a) the relationship between the citing and cited cases — are they the same dispute on appeal, or distinct cases meeting only through a citation? — and (b) who is acting — the citing case itself, or some third party the citing case is only reporting on. Severity — how harmful the treatment is to the cited case

Every treatment maps to one of four severity tiers. Severity is a deterministic lookup from the treatment label — not separately annotated — and is what most consumer surfaces (badges, facets, “is this still good law?” signals) display.

Stop

The cited case’s holding has been negated in whole or in significant part. Don’t rely on it without checking the current state. Examples: Reversed, Overruled, Abrogated, Vacated.

Warning

The cited case has been narrowed or contradicted. Some part still holds, but the safe use of it is materially smaller than the original opinion. Examples: Limited, Disapproved, Affirmed in part; Reversed in part.

Caution

The cited case is being noted with reservation, contrast, or modification. The holding still stands but the citing court takes some distance from it. Examples: Distinguished, Criticized, Declined to follow, Modified.

Neutral

The cited case is being acknowledged without negative treatment. Includes plain “Cited by” and a few neutral or positive procedural outcomes (Affirmed, Dismissed, Cert. denied).

A note on the “as recognized by” construction

“As recognized by” is the canonical phrasing courts use when they report that some other opinion already applied a treatment to a cited case — e.g. A newly published opinion states: “Roe v. Wade was overruled by Dobbs.” The treatment towards Roe v. Wade combines the underlying action (“Overruled”) with the modifier (“as recognized by”) to produce a Related Reference variant. There is no “Cited as recognized by” — merely citing a case is not a treatment, so it has no Related Reference form.
