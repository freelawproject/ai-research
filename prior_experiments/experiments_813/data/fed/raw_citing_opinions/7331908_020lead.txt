<opinion type="majority">
<author id="p-8">PAMELA K. CHEN, United States District Judge</author>
<p id="p-9">On January 4, 2012, Plaintiffs Patrick Hughes and Nafise Nina Hodjat initiated this putative class action against Defendants The Ester C Company, NBTY, Inc. ("NBTY"), and NatureSmart LLC, alleging <a class="page-label" data-citation-index="1" data-label="866" href="#p866" id="p866">*866</a>that Defendants' labeling of their "Ester-C" vitamin C supplements as "The Better Vitamin C" is unlawful, deceptive, and misbranded. On September 30, 2016, the Court denied Plaintiffs' motion to certify a nationwide class, as well as California and Missouri subclasses. Pending before the Court is Defendants' motion for summary judgment as to Plaintiffs' individual claims. For the reasons set forth below, the Court grants Defendants' motion for summary judgment in its entirety and dismisses this action.</p>
<p id="p-10"><strong>BACKGROUND</strong></p>
<p id="p-11"><strong>I. Factual Background</strong></p>
<p id="p-12">The Court presumes the parties' familiarity with the factual and procedural background of this case, which is detailed in the Court's prior orders resolving Defendants' motion to dismiss, <em>Hughes v. Ester C Co.,</em> <extracted-citation case-ids="3876462" index="0" url="https://cite.case.law/f-supp-2d/930/439/"><span class="citation" data-id="8706768"><a href="/opinion/8723572/hughes-v-ester-c-co/" aria-description="Citation for case: Hughes v. Ester C Co.">930 F.Supp.2d 439</a></span></extracted-citation> (E.D.N.Y. 2013),<footnotemark>2</footnotemark> Defendants' partial motion for summary judgment, <em>Hughes v. Ester C Co.,</em> <extracted-citation case-ids="5923810" index="1" url="https://cite.case.law/f-supp-3d/99/278/"><span class="citation" data-id="7230937"><a href="/opinion/7313032/hughes-v-ester-c-co/" aria-description="Citation for case: Hughes v. Ester C Co.">99 F.Supp.3d 278</a></span></extracted-citation> (E.D.N.Y. 2015), Plaintiffs' class certification motion, <em>Hughes v. Ester C Co.,</em> <extracted-citation case-ids="12124308" index="2" url="https://cite.case.law/frd/317/333/"><span class="citation" data-id="8776103"><a href="/opinion/8792111/hughes-v-ester-c-co-nbty/" aria-description="Citation for case: Hughes v. Ester C Co., NBTY">317 F.R.D. 333</a></span></extracted-citation> (E.D.N.Y. 2016), and Plaintiffs' motion for reconsideration of the denial of class certification, <em>Hughes v. Ester C Co.,</em> <extracted-citation case-ids="12309853" index="3" url="https://cite.case.law/frd/320/337/"><span class="citation" data-id="8777129"><a href="/opinion/8793122/hughes-v-ester-c-co/" aria-description="Citation for case: Hughes v. Ester C. Co.">320 F.R.D. 337</a></span></extracted-citation> (E.D.N.Y. 2017).</p>
<p id="p-13">Briefly, the Ester-C dietary supplements at issue in this action (the "Products") contain a patented form of vitamin C in the form of calcium ascorbate. (Defendants' 56.1 Statement ("Defs.' 56.1"), Dkt. 163, ¶ 4.)<footnotemark>3</footnotemark> According to Plaintiffs, misrepresentations in Ester-C's packaging and marketing create a reasonable expectation with purchasers that Ester-C provides a form of immune system defense that protects users from illnesses, and decreases one's likelihood of getting or remaining ill. (Amended Complaint ("Am. Compl."), Dkt. 13, at ¶¶ 2, 25; Plaintiff's Memorandum in Opposition to Partial Summary Judgment, ("Pl. Mem.") Dkt. 71, at ECF 6.<footnotemark>4</footnotemark> ) There is no dispute that NBTY manufactures and sells dietary supplements under the trademark "Ester-C®", that Ester-C products are sold in various forms, including tablets, gummies, and powder, and that the suggested retail price for Ester-C has been as low as 7 cents per grain and as high as 75 cents per gram. (Defs.' 56.1 ¶¶ 1-7.) While evidence of the amount of damages in the forrn of a price premium is disputed by the parties, it is not disputed that the amount in controversy for that price premium, as to Plaintiff Hughes's and Plaintiff Hodjat's individual purchases of Ester-C products, does not amount to $75,000. (<em>Compare</em> Defs.' 56.1 ¶ 8 to Plaintiffs' ("Pl.") 56.1, Dkt. 170-1, ¶ 8 (Sealed).)<footnotemark>5</footnotemark></p>
<p id="p-14">Plaintiffs do not dispute that they have adduced no extrinsic evidence of how consumers actually interpret Ester-C's "immune support" representation in isolation. (Defs.' 56.1 ¶ 19.) Plaintiffs have produced <a class="page-label" data-citation-index="1" data-label="867" href="#p867" id="p867">*867</a>no expert testimony or report concerning the immune benefits of vitamin C or lack thereof, the ability or inability of vitamin C to treat or prevent the common cold or influenza virus, or the relative bioavailability or absorbability of Ester-C and other forms of vitamin C, such as ascorbic acid. (<em>Id.</em> at ¶ 29). Plaintiffs proffer only a webpage on the servers of Oregon State University<footnotemark>6</footnotemark> and a journal article by Carol S. Johnston &amp; Bing Luo,<footnotemark>7</footnotemark> purportedly showing that vitamin C from Ester-C was not more "bioavailable" than simple ascorbic acid, and that there are conflicting studies as to whether Vitamin C enhances immune function. (Pl. 56.1 ¶ 17; Defs.' 56.1 Response to Pl. Additional Statement of Material Facts, Dkt. 167, ¶¶ 1-9; Plaintiffs' Memorandum of Law, ("Pl. Opp. Br."), Dkt. 170 (Sealed), at ECF 12.)</p>
<p id="p-15">Plaintiffs never testified that they saw or relied upon any of the purported implied disease claims on NBTY's website. (Defs.' 56.1 ¶ 67; Hodjat Dep. at 120:18-21, 192:6-193:6; Hughes Dep. at 77:5-7, 104:7-8; Defs.' 56.1 ¶ 69.) Plaintiff Hughes testified that he had already made up his mind to purchase Ester-C when he entered the store on the date of his sole purchase. (Defs.' 56.1 ¶ 60, Hughes Dep. at 77:5-7.)</p>

<p id="p-17">Defendants filed the instant motion for summary judgment on January 19, 2018. (Dkt. 161.) Plaintiffs filed their opposition, under seal, on February 26, 2018. Defendants filed their Reply Memorandum Brief with the Court earlier, on January 19, 2018, noting Plaintiffs' withdrawal of these claims. (Dkt. 166.) Thus, the remaining claims in this action are Plaintiffs' individual claims,<footnotemark>8</footnotemark> alleging: (1) as to Plaintiff Hodjat, violations of California's Consumer Legal Remedies Act ("CLRA"), <extracted-citation index="4" url="https://cite.case.law/citations/?q=Cal.%20Code%20%C2%A7%201750"><span class="citation no-link">Cal. Civ. Code § 1750</span></extracted-citation>, False Advertising Law ("FAL"), Cal. Bus. and Prof. Code § 17500, <em>et seq.</em> , Unfair Competition Law ("UCL"), Cal. Bus. and Prof. Code § 17200, <em>et seq.</em> , and the California "Sherman Law", <extracted-citation index="5" url="https://cite.case.law/citations/?q=Cal.%20Code%20%C2%A7%20109875"><span class="citation no-link">Cal. Health &amp; Safety Code § 109875</span></extracted-citation>, <em>et seq.</em> ; (2) as to Plaintiff Hughes, violations of Missouri's Merchandising Practices Act ("MMPA"), <extracted-citation index="6" url="https://cite.case.law/citations/?q=Mo.%20Rev.%20Stat.%20%C2%A7%20407.010"><span class="citation no-link">Mo. Rev. Stat. § 407.010</span></extracted-citation> ; and (3) as to both Plaintiffs, a New York common law "unjust enrichment" claim.</p>
<p id="p-18"><strong>LEGAL STANDARD</strong></p>
<p id="p-19">Summary judgment may be granted only where there is no genuine issue as to any material fact and the moving party is entitled to a judgment as a matter of law. Fed. R. Civ. P. 56(a). In ruling on a motion for summary judgment, a court must resolve all ambiguities and draw all factual inferences in favor of the nonmoving party. <em>McClellan v. Smith</em> , <extracted-citation case-ids="31240" index="7" url="https://cite.case.law/f3d/439/137/#p144"><span class="citation" data-id="8409903"><a href="/opinion/8439183/mcclellan-v-smith/" aria-description="Citation for case: McClellan v. Smith">439 F.3d 137</a></span></extracted-citation>, 144 (2d Cir. 2006). "To grant the motion, the court must determine that there is no genuine issue of material fact to be tried." <em><extracted-citation case-ids="31240" index="8" url="https://cite.case.law/f3d/439/137/#p144"><span class="citation" data-id="8409903"><a href="/opinion/8439183/mcclellan-v-smith/" aria-description="Citation for case: McClellan v. Smith">Id.</a></span></extracted-citation></em> (citing <a class="page-label" data-citation-index="1" data-label="868" href="#p868" id="p868">*868</a><em>Celotex Corp. v. Catrett,</em> <extracted-citation case-ids="6207800" index="9" url="https://cite.case.law/us/477/317/#p322"><span class="citation" data-id="9430607"><a href="/opinion/111722/celotex-corp-v-catrett-administratrix-of-the-estate-of-catrett/" aria-description="Citation for case: Celotex Corp. v. Catrett, Administratrix of the Estate of...">477 U.S. 317</a></span></extracted-citation>, 322-23, <extracted-citation case-ids="6207800" index="10" url="https://cite.case.law/us/477/317/#p322"><span class="citation" data-id="9430607"><a href="/opinion/111722/celotex-corp-v-catrett-administratrix-of-the-estate-of-catrett/" aria-description="Citation for case: Celotex Corp. v. Catrett, Administratrix of the Estate of...">106 S.Ct. 2548</a></span></extracted-citation>, <extracted-citation case-ids="6207800" index="11" url="https://cite.case.law/us/477/317/#p322"><span class="citation" data-id="9430607"><a href="/opinion/111722/celotex-corp-v-catrett-administratrix-of-the-estate-of-catrett/" aria-description="Citation for case: Celotex Corp. v. Catrett, Administratrix of the Estate of...">91 L.Ed.2d 265</a></span></extracted-citation> (1986) ). A genuine factual issue exists where the "evidence is such that a reasonable jury could return a verdict for the nonmoving party." <em>Anderson v. Liberty Lobby, Inc.,</em> <extracted-citation case-ids="6206897" index="12" url="https://cite.case.law/us/477/242/#p248"><span class="citation" data-id="9430599"><a href="/opinion/111719/anderson-v-liberty-lobby-inc/" aria-description="Citation for case: Anderson v. Liberty Lobby, Inc.">477 U.S. 242</a></span></extracted-citation>, 248, <extracted-citation case-ids="6206897" index="13" url="https://cite.case.law/us/477/242/#p248"><span class="citation" data-id="9430599"><a href="/opinion/111719/anderson-v-liberty-lobby-inc/" aria-description="Citation for case: Anderson v. Liberty Lobby, Inc.">106 S.Ct. 2505</a></span></extracted-citation>, <extracted-citation case-ids="6206897" index="14" url="https://cite.case.law/us/477/242/#p248"><span class="citation" data-id="9430599"><a href="/opinion/111719/anderson-v-liberty-lobby-inc/" aria-description="Citation for case: Anderson v. Liberty Lobby, Inc.">91 L.Ed.2d 202</a></span></extracted-citation> (1986).</p>
<p id="p-20">The initial burden of "establishing the absence of any genuine issue of material fact" rests with the moving party. <em>Zalaski v. City of Bridgeport Police Dep't</em> , <extracted-citation case-ids="3703148" index="15" url="https://cite.case.law/f3d/613/336/#p340"><span class="citation" data-id="9436738"><a href="/opinion/151612/zalaski-v-city-of-bridgeport-police-department/" aria-description="Citation for case: Zalaski v. City of Bridgeport Police Department">613 F.3d 336</a></span></extracted-citation>, 340 (2d Cir. 2010). Once this burden is met, however, the burden shifts to the nonmoving party to put forward some evidence establishing the existence of a question of fact that must be resolved at trial. <em>Spinelli v. City of N.Y.</em> , <extracted-citation case-ids="4053909" index="16" url="https://cite.case.law/f3d/579/160/#p166"><span class="citation" data-id="2490"><a href="/opinion/2490/spinelli-v-city-of-new-york/" aria-description="Citation for case: Spinelli v. City of New York">579 F.3d 160</a></span></extracted-citation>, 166-67 (2d Cir. 2009) ; <em>see also</em> <em>Celotex Corp.</em> , <span class="citation" data-id="9430607"><a href="/opinion/111722/celotex-corp-v-catrett-administratrix-of-the-estate-of-catrett/#322" aria-description="Citation for case: Celotex Corp. v. Catrett, Administratrix of the Estate of...">477 U.S. at 322-23</a></span>, <extracted-citation case-ids="6207800" index="17" url="https://cite.case.law/us/477/317/#p322"><span class="citation" data-id="9430607"><a href="/opinion/111722/celotex-corp-v-catrett-administratrix-of-the-estate-of-catrett/" aria-description="Citation for case: Celotex Corp. v. Catrett, Administratrix of the Estate of...">106 S.Ct. 2548</a></span></extracted-citation>. A mere "scintilla of evidence" in support of the nonmoving party is insufficient; "there must be evidence on which the jury could reasonably find for the [non-movant]." <em>Hayut v. State Univ. of N.Y.</em> , <extracted-citation case-ids="9297719" index="18" url="https://cite.case.law/f3d/352/733/#p743"><span class="citation multiple-matches"><a href="/c/F.3d/352/733/">352 F.3d 733</a></span></extracted-citation>, 743 (2d Cir. 2003) (quotation omitted; alterations in original). In other words, "[t]he nonmoving party must come forward with specific facts showing that there is a genuine issue for trial." <em>Caldarola v. Calabrese</em> , <extracted-citation case-ids="11448867" index="19" url="https://cite.case.law/f3d/298/156/#p160"><span class="citation" data-id="7012402"><a href="/opinion/7106428/caldarola-v-calabrese/" aria-description="Citation for case: Caldarola v. Calabrese">298 F.3d 156</a></span></extracted-citation>, 160 (2d Cir. 2002) (quotation omitted). In determining whether a genuine issue of fact exists, the court must resolve all ambiguities and draw all reasonable inferences against the moving party. <em>Major League Baseball Props., Inc. v. Salvino, Inc.</em> , <extracted-citation case-ids="3841394" index="20" url="https://cite.case.law/f3d/542/290/#p309"><span class="citation" data-id="9413324"><a href="/opinion/2583/major-league-baseball-properties-inc-v-salvino-inc/" aria-description="Citation for case: Major League Baseball Properties, Inc. v. Salvino, Inc.">542 F.3d 290</a></span></extracted-citation>, 309 (2d Cir. 2008).</p>
<p id="p-21">However, "the mere existence of some alleged factual dispute between the parties will not defeat an otherwise properly supported motion for summary judgment." <em>Anderson</em> , <span class="citation" data-id="9430599"><a href="/opinion/111719/anderson-v-liberty-lobby-inc/#247" aria-description="Citation for case: Anderson v. Liberty Lobby, Inc.">477 U.S. at 247-48</a></span>, <extracted-citation case-ids="6206897" index="21" url="https://cite.case.law/us/477/242/#p248"><span class="citation" data-id="9430599"><a href="/opinion/111719/anderson-v-liberty-lobby-inc/" aria-description="Citation for case: Anderson v. Liberty Lobby, Inc.">106 S.Ct. 2505</a></span></extracted-citation>. "[What] is required [from a nonmoving party] is that sufficient evidence supporting the claimed factual dispute be shown to require a jury or judge to resolve the parties' differing versions of the truth at trial." <em>First Nat'l Bank of Ariz. v. Cities Serv. Co.,</em> <extracted-citation case-ids="1767758" index="22" url="https://cite.case.law/us/391/253/#p288"><span class="citation" data-id="9423704"><a href="/opinion/107691/first-nat-bank-of-ariz-v-cities-service-co/" aria-description="Citation for case: First Nat. Bank of Ariz. v. Cities Service Co.">391 U.S. 253</a></span></extracted-citation>, 288-89, <extracted-citation case-ids="1767758" index="23" url="https://cite.case.law/us/391/253/#p288"><span class="citation" data-id="9423704"><a href="/opinion/107691/first-nat-bank-of-ariz-v-cities-service-co/" aria-description="Citation for case: First Nat. Bank of Ariz. v. Cities Service Co.">88 S.Ct. 1575</a></span></extracted-citation>, <extracted-citation case-ids="1767758" index="24" url="https://cite.case.law/us/391/253/#p288"><span class="citation" data-id="9423704"><a href="/opinion/107691/first-nat-bank-of-ariz-v-cities-service-co/" aria-description="Citation for case: First Nat. Bank of Ariz. v. Cities Service Co.">20 L.Ed.2d 569</a></span></extracted-citation> (1968). "Credibility assessments, choices between conflicting versions of the events, and the weighing of evidence are matters for the jury, not for the court on a motion for summary judgment." <em>Fischl v. Armitage,</em> <extracted-citation case-ids="978134" index="25" url="https://cite.case.law/f3d/128/50/#p55"><span class="citation" data-id="747591"><a href="/opinion/747591/josef-fischl-v-david-armitage-corrections-sergeant-and-sa-marshall/" aria-description="Citation for case: Josef Fischl v. David Armitage, Corrections Sergeant, and...">128 F.3d 50</a></span></extracted-citation>, 55 (2d Cir. 1997).</p>
<p id="p-22"><strong>DISCUSSION</strong></p>
<p id="p-23"><strong>I. Subject Matter Jurisdiction</strong></p>
<p id="p-24">As a preliminary matter, the Court notes that because Plaintiffs' motion for class certification was denied and they are not asserting diversity jurisdiction under 28 U.S.C. 1332(a) (<em>see</em> Pl. Opp. Br., Dkt. 170 at ECF 17, n. 7), it is unclear whether the Court retains subject matter jurisdiction over this action. <em>See</em> <em>Gagasoules v. MBF Leasing LLC</em> , <extracted-citation case-ids="4252280" index="26" url="https://cite.case.law/frd/286/205/#p209"><span class="citation" data-id="8766293"><a href="/opinion/8782527/gagasoules-v-mbf-leasing-llc/" aria-description="Citation for case: Gagasoules v. MBF Leasing LLC">286 F.R.D. 205</a></span></extracted-citation>, 209-10 (E.D.N.Y. 2012) ("[T]he Second Circuit has not addressed whether subject matter jurisdiction survives a denial of class certification absent diversity or federal question jurisdiction."). Given the uncertainty on this issue in this Circuit,<footnotemark>9</footnotemark> the Court <a class="page-label" data-citation-index="1" data-label="869" href="#p869" id="p869">*869</a>declines to resolve Defendants' summary judgment motion on the basis of subject matter jurisdiction and instead decides the motion on the merits.</p>
<p id="p-25"><strong>II. Plaintiffs' Claims</strong></p>
<p id="p-26">Plaintiffs allege that two categories of statements that have appeared in Ester-C's labeling constitute false advertising and misrepresentations that violate California and Missouri law: (1) statements about "immune support"; and (2) the slogan, "The Better Vitamin C ®". (Am. Compl., ¶¶ 3, 5, 17, 22, 23, 56, 62.)</p>
<p id="p-27">In Count I of the Amended Complaint, Plaintiff Hughes asserts that these allegedly false statements constitute a violation of Section 2 of the MMPA, <extracted-citation index="27" url="https://cite.case.law/citations/?q=Mo.%20Rev.%20Stat.%20%C2%A7%20407.010"><span class="citation no-link">Mo. Rev. Stat. § 407.010</span></extracted-citation>, which provides:</p>
<blockquote id="p-28">The act, use or employment by any person of any deception, fraud, false pretense, false promise, misrepresentation, unfair practice or the concealment, suppression, or omission of any material fact in connection with the sale or advertisement of any merchandise in trade or commerce ... in or from the state of Missouri, is declared to be an unlawful practice.... Any act, use or employment declared unlawful by this subsection violates this subsection whether committed before, during or after the sale advertisement or solicitation.</blockquote>
<p id="p-29">In Count II of the Amended Complaint, Plaintiff Hodjat asserts that these statements violate the CLRA's prohibition on "unfair methods of competition and unfair or deceptive acts or practices." <extracted-citation index="28" url="https://cite.case.law/citations/?q=Cal.%20Code%20%C2%A7%201770"><span class="citation no-link">Cal. Civ. Code § 1770</span></extracted-citation>. In Count III, she alleges that these statements constitute "unfair, deceptive, untrue, or misleading advertising" that are prohibited by the FAL. Cal. Bus. and Prof. Code § 17500.</p>
<p id="p-30">In Count IV, Hodjat appears to allege five separate violations of the UCL, which proscribes "unlawful, unfair[,] or fraudulent business act or practice[s]." <extracted-citation index="29" url="https://cite.case.law/citations/?q=Cal.%20Code%20%C2%A7%2017200"><span class="citation no-link">Cal. Bus. &amp; Prof Code § 17200</span></extracted-citation>. "Each prong of the UCL is a separate and distinct theory of liability." <em>Kearns v. Ford Motor Co.</em> , <extracted-citation case-ids="3652195" index="30" url="https://cite.case.law/f3d/567/1120/#p1127"><span class="citation" data-id="1208595"><a href="/opinion/1208595/kearns-v-ford-motor-co/" aria-description="Citation for case: Kearns v. Ford Motor Co.">567 F.3d 1120</a></span></extracted-citation>, 1127 (9th Cir. 2009). The UCL also "borrows" predicate legal violations and treats them as "independently actionable" under the unlawful prong of the UCL. <em>Wang v. Massey Chevrolet</em> , <extracted-citation case-ids="1066686" index="31" url="https://cite.case.law/cal-app-4th/97/856/#p871"><span class="citation" data-id="2294333"><a href="/opinion/2294333/wang-v-massey-chevrolet/" aria-description="Citation for case: Wang v. Massey Chevrolet">97 Cal. App. 4th 856</a></span></extracted-citation>, 871, <extracted-citation index="32" url="https://cite.case.law/citations/?q=118%20Cal.%20Rptr.%202d%20770"><span class="citation" data-id="2294333"><a href="/opinion/2294333/wang-v-massey-chevrolet/" aria-description="Citation for case: Wang v. Massey Chevrolet">118 Cal.Rptr.2d 770</a></span></extracted-citation> (2002). Hodjat's first two UCL claims in Count IV thus "borrow" or incorporate deceptive practice and false advertising claims from the CLRA and FAL, respectively, under the "unlawful" prong of the UCL. (<em>See</em> Am. Compl. ¶ 67.) Hodjat's third claim under the UCL "borrows" or incorporates violations of several provisions of California's Sherman Law, which, <em>inter alia</em> , proscribes "misbranding" of food and drug products, under the "unlawful" prong of the UCL.<footnotemark>10</footnotemark> (Am. Compl.</p>
<p id="p-31"><a class="page-label" data-citation-index="1" data-label="870" href="#p870" id="p870">*870</a>¶¶ 68-69.) Finally, Hodjat alleges a violation of the "fraudulent" prong of the UCL, which "requires a showing [that] members of the public are likely to be deceived", <em>Wang,</em> <extracted-citation case-ids="1066686" index="33" url="https://cite.case.law/cal-app-4th/97/856/#p871"><span class="citation" data-id="2294333"><a href="/opinion/2294333/wang-v-massey-chevrolet/" aria-description="Citation for case: Wang v. Massey Chevrolet">97 Cal. App. 4th at 871</a></span></extracted-citation>, <extracted-citation index="34" url="https://cite.case.law/citations/?q=118%20Cal.%20Rptr.%202d%20770"><span class="citation" data-id="2294333"><a href="/opinion/2294333/wang-v-massey-chevrolet/" aria-description="Citation for case: Wang v. Massey Chevrolet">118 Cal.Rptr.2d 770</a></span></extracted-citation>, and a separate violation under the "unfair" prong of the UCL, alleging false advertising, consumer deception, and misrepresentation as the unfair practice at issue.<footnotemark>11</footnotemark> (Am. Compl. ¶¶ 70-71, 72-76.)</p>
<p id="p-32"><strong>III. Plaintiffs' False Advertising and Misrepresentation Claims</strong></p>
<p id="p-33">The elements of Plaintiffs' false advertising and misrepresentation claims under Missouri and California law substantially overlap. To state a claim under the MMPA, a plaintiff must show that (1) [he] purchased the merchandise in question; (2) [he] purchased the merchandise for personal, family, or household use; (3) [he] suffered an ascertainable loss; and (4) the ascertainable loss was the result of an unfair practice." <em>Bryan v. Bank of Am., N.A.</em> , No. 4:17-CV-1616 (RLW), <extracted-citation index="35" url="https://cite.case.law/citations/?q=2018%20WL%20485968"><span class="citation no-link">2018 WL 485968</span></extracted-citation>, at *4 (E.D. Mo. Jan. 18, 2018) (internal quotation omitted); <em>Snelling v. HSBC Card Servs., Inc.</em> , No. 4:14-CV-431 (CDP), <extracted-citation index="36" url="https://cite.case.law/citations/?q=2015%20WL%20457949"><span class="citation no-link">2015 WL 457949</span></extracted-citation>, at *9 (E.D. Mo. Feb. 3, 2015) (to satisfy fourth element of MMPA claim, plaintiff must allege facts establishing that defendants used or employed a deception, fraud, false pretense, false promise, misrepresentation in connection with the purchase of the product). To prevail under the UCL, FAL, and the CLRA, a plaintiff must demonstrate that she "actually relied on the challenged misrepresentations and suffered economic injury as a result of that reliance."</p>
<p id="p-34"><a class="page-label" data-citation-index="1" data-label="871" href="#p871" id="p871">*871</a><em>Wilson v. Frito-Lay N. Am., Inc.,</em> <extracted-citation case-ids="12273384" index="37" url="https://cite.case.law/f-supp-3d/260/1202/#p1208"><span class="citation" data-id="7244143"><a href="/opinion/7326229/wilson-v-frito-lay-north-america-inc/" aria-description="Citation for case: Wilson v. Frito-Lay North America, Inc.">260 F.Supp.3d 1202</a></span></extracted-citation>, 1208 (N.D. Cal. 2017) (internal citations omitted). Thus, Plaintiffs' false advertising and misrepresentation claims under Missouri and California law share the following core elements: (1) that Defendants' labeling conveyed the allegedly false or misleading implied statements; (2) that the implied statements are, in fact, false or misleading;<footnotemark>12</footnotemark> (3) that the implied statements were the motivating cause of Plaintiffs' purchase decisions; and (4) that Plaintiffs suffered damages or economic injury, here, in the form of a price premium.</p>
<p id="p-35"><strong>A. Plaintiffs Cannot Demonstrate that Ester-C's Labeling Conveyed the Alleged Implied Disease Claims</strong></p>
<p id="p-36">Plaintiffs' false advertising and misrepresentation claims are based on the following allegedly false implied statements: (1) "immune support" statements that, when viewed in combination with other statements contained in the labeling of Ester-C, convey the false message that Ester-C is capable of preventing or treating influenza or the common cold (Pl. 56.1 ¶ 19); and (2) "The Better Vitamin C ®" slogan that, in context, conveys a false message that Ester-C is more effectively absorbed into the human body than ascorbic acid. (Pl. Opp. Br. at ECF 12, 20-21.) Because Plaintiffs provide no evidence that a reasonable consumer perceived Defendants' labeling as conveying these implied disease claims, they cannot prove the first element of their false advertising and misrepresentation causes of action.</p>
<p id="p-37">Under the CLRA, UCL, FAL, and MMPA, it is not enough for a plaintiff to assert, based on his or her own subjective belief that a statement on the defendant's label conveyed the alleged implied message. Rather, false advertising claims under these statutes are governed by the "reasonable consumer" test. <em>Ebner v. Fresh, Inc.</em> , <extracted-citation case-ids="12174299" index="38" url="https://cite.case.law/f3d/838/958/#p965"><span class="citation" data-id="8414293"><a href="/opinion/8442970/ebner-v-fresh-inc/" aria-description="Citation for case: Ebner v. Fresh, Inc.">838 F.3d 958</a></span></extracted-citation>, 965 (9th Cir. 2016) ; <em>Maxwell v. Unilever United States, Inc.</em> , No. 5:12-CV-01736 (EJD), <extracted-citation index="39" url="https://cite.case.law/citations/?q=2018%20WL%201536761"><span class="citation no-link">2018 WL 1536761</span></extracted-citation>, at *5 (N.D. Cal. Mar. 29, 2018) ("False advertising claims under the FAL, the CLRA, and the fraudulent and unfair prongs of the UCL are governed by the 'reasonable consumer' standard."); <em>Ries v. Arizona Beverages USA LLC</em> , No. 10-CV-01139 (RS), <extracted-citation index="40" url="https://cite.case.law/citations/?q=2013%20WL%201287416"><span class="citation no-link">2013 WL 1287416</span></extracted-citation>, *3 (N.D. Cal. Mar. 28, 2013) ("UCL, FAL, and CLRA claims grounded in fraud are governed by the 'reasonable consumer test.' "); <em>Williams v. Gerber Products Co.</em> , <extracted-citation case-ids="4012495" index="41" url="https://cite.case.law/f3d/552/934/#p938"><span class="citation" data-id="1243130"><a href="/opinion/1243130/williams-v-gerber-products-co/" aria-description="Citation for case: Williams v. Gerber Products Co.">552 F.3d 934</a></span></extracted-citation>, 938 (9th Cir. 2008) (same); <em>Lavie v. Procter &amp; Gamble Co.</em> , <extracted-citation case-ids="301020" index="42" url="https://cite.case.law/cal-app-4th/105/496/#p504"><span class="citation" data-id="9749498"><a href="/opinion/2283365/lavie-v-procter-gamble-co/" aria-description="Citation for case: Lavie v. Procter &amp; Gamble Co.">105 Cal. App. 4th 496</a></span></extracted-citation>, 504, <extracted-citation index="43" url="https://cite.case.law/citations/?q=129%20Cal.%20Rptr.%202d%20486"><span class="citation" data-id="9749498"><a href="/opinion/2283365/lavie-v-procter-gamble-co/" aria-description="Citation for case: Lavie v. Procter &amp; Gamble Co.">129 Cal.Rptr.2d 486</a></span></extracted-citation> (2003) (same); <em>Kasky</em> , 27 Cal. 4th at 951, <extracted-citation index="44" url="https://cite.case.law/citations/?q=119%20Cal.%20Rptr.%202d%20296"><span class="citation" data-id="9797673"><a href="/opinion/2638712/kasky-v-nike-inc/" aria-description="Citation for case: Kasky v. Nike, Inc.">119 Cal.Rptr.2d 296</a></span></extracted-citation>, <extracted-citation index="45" url="https://cite.case.law/citations/?q=45%20P.3d%20243"><span class="citation multiple-matches"><a href="/c/P.3d/45/243/">45 P.3d 243</a></span></extracted-citation> (same); <em>Hess v. Chase Manhattan Bank, USA, N.A.</em> , <extracted-citation case-ids="8312436" index="46" url="https://cite.case.law/sw3d/220/758/#p773"><span class="citation" data-id="9678131"><a href="/opinion/1755829/hess-v-chase-manhattan-bank-usa-na/" aria-description="Citation for case: Hess v. Chase Manhattan Bank, USA, N.A.">220 S.W.3d 758</a></span></extracted-citation>, 773 (Mo. 2007) ("reasonable consumer" standard also applies under MMPA).</p>
<p id="p-38">Under the "reasonable consumer" standard, Plaintiffs must prove that Defendants' statements were, in fact, conveyed to "a significant portion of the general consuming public ... acting reasonably in the circumstances." <em>Ebner</em> , <extracted-citation case-ids="12174299" index="47" url="https://cite.case.law/f3d/838/958/#p965">838 F.3d at </extracted-citation>965 ; <em>Hess,</em> <extracted-citation case-ids="8312436" index="48" url="https://cite.case.law/sw3d/220/758/#p773"><span class="citation" data-id="9678131"><a href="/opinion/1755829/hess-v-chase-manhattan-bank-usa-na/" aria-description="Citation for case: Hess v. Chase Manhattan Bank, USA, N.A.">220 S.W.3d at 773</a></span></extracted-citation>. This standard requires more than a mere possibility that Defendants' label "might conceivably be misunderstood by some few consumers viewing it in an unreasonable manner." <em>Ebner</em> , <extracted-citation case-ids="12174299" index="49" url="https://cite.case.law/f3d/838/958/#p965"><span class="citation" data-id="8414293"><a href="/opinion/8442970/ebner-v-fresh-inc/" aria-description="Citation for case: Ebner v. Fresh, Inc.">838 F.3d at 965</a></span></extracted-citation> (quoting <a class="page-label" data-citation-index="1" data-label="872" href="#p872" id="p872">*872</a><em>Lavie</em> , <extracted-citation case-ids="301020" index="50" url="https://cite.case.law/cal-app-4th/105/496/#p504"><span class="citation" data-id="9749498"><a href="/opinion/2283365/lavie-v-procter-gamble-co/" aria-description="Citation for case: Lavie v. Procter &amp; Gamble Co.">105 Cal. App. 4th 496</a></span></extracted-citation>, <extracted-citation index="51" url="https://cite.case.law/citations/?q=129%20Cal.%20Rptr.%202d%20486"><span class="citation" data-id="9749498"><a href="/opinion/2283365/lavie-v-procter-gamble-co/" aria-description="Citation for case: Lavie v. Procter &amp; Gamble Co.">129 Cal.Rptr.2d 486</a></span></extracted-citation> ); <em>Maxwell,</em> <extracted-citation index="52" url="https://cite.case.law/citations/?q=2018%20WL%201536761"><span class="citation no-link">2018 WL 1536761</span></extracted-citation>, at *4-6 (same). "Rather, the reasonable consumer standard requires a probability that a significant portion of the general consuming public or of targeted consumers, acting reasonably in the circumstances, could be misled." <em>Ebner</em> , <extracted-citation case-ids="12174299" index="53" url="https://cite.case.law/f3d/838/958/#p965"><span class="citation" data-id="8414293"><a href="/opinion/8442970/ebner-v-fresh-inc/#965" aria-description="Citation for case: Ebner v. Fresh, Inc.">838 F.3d at 965</a></span></extracted-citation> (internal citations and quotation marks omitted). To satisfy the reasonable consumer standard, a plaintiff must adduce extrinsic evidence-ordinarily in the form of a survey<footnotemark>13</footnotemark> -to show how reasonable consumers interpret the challenged claims. <em>Ries,</em> <extracted-citation index="54" url="https://cite.case.law/citations/?q=2013%20WL%201287416"><span class="citation no-link">2013 WL 1287416</span></extracted-citation>, at *6 ("[A]necdotal evidence alone is insufficient ... [T]o prevail, plaintiff must demonstrate by extrinsic evidence, such as consumer survey evidence, that the challenged statements tend to mislead [reasonable] consumers."); <em>Morales v. Conopco, Inc.</em> , No. 2:13-CV-2213 (WBS) (EFB), <extracted-citation index="55" url="https://cite.case.law/citations/?q=2016%20WL%206094504"><span class="citation no-link">2016 WL 6094504</span></extracted-citation>, at *4 (E.D. Cal. Oct. 18, 2016) ("[Plaintiffs'] individual testimony is insufficient to establish whether defendant's representations on its products would deceive a reasonable consumer....") (citing <em>Clemens v. DaimlerChrysler Corp.</em> , <extracted-citation case-ids="4065346" index="56" url="https://cite.case.law/f3d/534/1017/#p1025"><span class="citation" data-id="1199775"><a href="/opinion/1199775/clemens-v-daimlerchrysler-corp/" aria-description="Citation for case: Clemens v. DaimlerChrysler Corp.">534 F.3d 1017</a></span></extracted-citation>, 1025-26 (9th Cir. 2008) ).</p>
<p id="p-39">Here, Plaintiffs offer nothing more than their own conclusory allegations and "anecdotal" testimony to show what implied claims Ester-C purportedly conveyed. This is plainly insufficient to create a genuine issue of material fact as to whether the statements on Ester-C's labeling conveyed the alleged implied disease claims. The record otherwise contains no evidence-extrinsic or otherwise-that a broad cross-section of reasonable consumers interpret the challenged statements in the manner Plaintiffs allege.</p>
<p id="p-40">Accordingly, Defendants are entitled to summary judgment solely on this ground with respect to Plaintiffs' false advertising and misrepresentation claims under the CLRA, FAL, UCL, and MMPA.</p>
<p id="p-41"><strong>B. Plaintiffs Cannot Show That Any Implied Disease Claims on Ester-C's Labeling are Materially False or Misleading</strong></p>
<p id="p-42">Plaintiffs also cannot establish the second element of their false advertising and misrepresentation claims-namely, that the alleged implied disease claims on Ester-C's labeling are, in fact, materially false or misleading. Because vitamin C's contribution to human immune function and the relative bioavailability of different molecular forms of vitamin C are subjects "beyond the ken of the average layperson," Plaintiffs are "unable to sustain their burden of pro[ving falsity] ... without presenting <a class="page-label" data-citation-index="1" data-label="873" href="#p873" id="p873">*873</a>[expert] testimony from a person with the requisite technical and scientific knowledge." <em>In re Cokes,</em> <extracted-citation case-ids="9111422,9111383" index="57" url="https://cite.case.law/sw3d/107/317/"><span class="citation" data-id="1637522"><a href="/opinion/1637522/in-re-the-care-treatment-of-cokes/" aria-description="Citation for case: In Re the Care &amp; Treatment of Cokes">107 S.W.3d 317</a></span></extracted-citation>, 323-24 (Mo. Ct. App. 2003) ; <em>Chandler v. Janssen Pharmaceuticals, Inc.,</em> No. 15-CV-3106 (PKC) (SJB), <extracted-citation case-ids="12526420" index="58" url="https://cite.case.law/f-supp-3d/322/314/#p325"><span class="citation" data-id="7249159"><a href="/opinion/7331241/chandler-v-janssen-pharm-inc/" aria-description="Citation for case: Chandler v. Janssen Pharm., Inc.">322 F.Supp.3d 314</a></span></extracted-citation>, 325, <extracted-citation index="59" url="https://cite.case.law/citations/?q=2018%20WL%203212422"><span class="citation no-link">2018 WL 3212422</span></extracted-citation>, at *8 (E.D.N.Y. Jun. 29, 2018) ("expert testimony is required when the factual content of the underlying issues is not found within common knowledge and experience of laypersons."); <em>see also</em> <em>Zakaria v. Gerber Prod. Co.</em> , No. LA-CV-01500-2000 (JAK) (EX), <extracted-citation index="60" url="https://cite.case.law/citations/?q=2015%20WL%204379743"><span class="citation no-link">2015 WL 4379743</span></extracted-citation>, at *3 (C.D. Cal. Jul. 14, 2015) (citing <em>Nat'l Council Against Health Fraud, Inc. v. King Bio Pharm., Inc.,</em> <extracted-citation case-ids="204694" index="61" url="https://cite.case.law/cal-app-4th/107/1336/#p1345"><span class="citation" data-id="2261872"><a href="/opinion/2261872/national-council-against-health-fraud-inc-v-king-bio-pharmaceuticals/" aria-description="Citation for case: National Council Against Health Fraud, Inc. v. King Bio...">107 Cal. App. 4th 1336</a></span></extracted-citation>, 1345, <extracted-citation index="62" url="https://cite.case.law/citations/?q=133%20Cal.%20Rptr.%202d%20207"><span class="citation" data-id="2261872"><a href="/opinion/2261872/national-council-against-health-fraud-inc-v-king-bio-pharmaceuticals/" aria-description="Citation for case: National Council Against Health Fraud, Inc. v. King Bio...">133 Cal.Rptr.2d 207</a></span></extracted-citation> (2003) ) ("To be sure, where the scientific evidence is inconclusive, a plaintiff may fail to carry her burden to show actual falsehood, and a private false advertising claim cannot be based on mere lack of substantiation.").</p>
<p id="p-43">Here, Plaintiffs have disclosed no expert testimony or report concerning the immune benefits of vitamin C or lack thereof, or the ability or inability of vitamin C to treat or prevent the common cold or influenza virus. (Defs.' 56.1 ¶ 23.) The scientific reports Plaintiffs rely on, namely, the Oregon State Webpage and the Johnston study article, are plainly inadmissible hearsay. <em>Cruz v. City of New Rochelle,</em> No. 13-CV-7432 (LMS), <extracted-citation index="63" url="https://cite.case.law/citations/?q=2017%20WL%201402122"><span class="citation no-link">2017 WL 1402122</span></extracted-citation>, at *24 (S.D.N.Y. Apr. 3, 2017) (published internet articles inadmissible on summary judgment) (citing <em>Glowczenski v. Taser Int'l, Inc.,</em> <extracted-citation case-ids="4040008" index="64" url="https://cite.case.law/f-supp-2d/928/564/#p572"><span class="citation" data-id="8706519"><a href="/opinion/8723323/glowczenski-v-taser-international-inc/" aria-description="Citation for case: Glowczenski v. Taser International, Inc.">928 F.Supp.2d 564</a></span></extracted-citation>, 572 (E.D.N.Y. 2013) ("A published article is hearsay.... [T]he potential establishment of the articles at trial as reliable authorities does not make them admissible as evidence on the current motion for summary judgment."), <em>aff'd in part, dismissed in part on other grounds</em> , <span class="citation" data-id="8676804"><a href="/opinion/8693710/glowczenski-v-taser-international-inc/" aria-description="Citation for case: Glowczenski v. Taser International Inc.">594 Fed. App'x 723</a></span> (2d Cir. 2014) ).</p>
<p id="p-44">Neither the Johnston study nor the Oregon State Webpage can be admitted without an expert to testify as to their reliability and acceptance in the relevant field of expertise. <em>Schneider v. Revici</em> , <extracted-citation case-ids="1670957" index="65" url="https://cite.case.law/f2d/817/987/#p991"><span class="citation" data-id="487744"><a href="/opinion/487744/edith-schneider-and-herman-schneider-v-emanuel-revici-md-and-institute/" aria-description="Citation for case: Edith Schneider and Herman Schneider v. Emanuel Revici,...">817 F.2d 987</a></span></extracted-citation>, 991 (2d Cir. 1987) (Federal Rule of Evidence "803(18) explicitly requires that to qualify under the learned treatise exception, a proper foundation as to the authoritativeness of the text must be laid by an expert witness"). Plaintiffs offer no such expert. <em>Chandler</em> , <extracted-citation case-ids="12526420" index="66" url="https://cite.case.law/f-supp-3d/322/314/#p325"><span class="citation" data-id="7249159"><a href="/opinion/7331241/chandler-v-janssen-pharm-inc/" aria-description="Citation for case: Chandler v. Janssen Pharm., Inc.">322 F.Supp.3d at 325</a></span>-26</extracted-citation>, <extracted-citation index="67" url="https://cite.case.law/citations/?q=2018%20WL%203212422"><span class="citation no-link">2018 WL 3212422</span></extracted-citation>, at *8 (finding that absence of expert to interpret and validate relevant studies precluded their admission); <em>see also</em> <em>Schneider,</em> <extracted-citation case-ids="1670957" index="68" url="https://cite.case.law/f2d/817/987/#p991">817 F.2d at </extracted-citation>991 ; <em>Glowczenski,</em> <extracted-citation case-ids="4040008" index="69" url="https://cite.case.law/f-supp-2d/928/564/#p572"><span class="citation" data-id="8706519"><a href="/opinion/8723323/glowczenski-v-taser-international-inc/" aria-description="Citation for case: Glowczenski v. Taser International, Inc.">928 F.Supp.2d at 572</a></span>-73</extracted-citation>.<footnotemark>14</footnotemark></p>
<p id="p-45">Plaintiffs have similarly failed to offer any evidence to show the material falsity of the slogan, "The Better Vitamin C ®," which Plaintiffs allege conveys the implied statement that Ester-C is more effectively absorbed into the human body than ascorbic acid. (Defs.' 56.1 ¶ 24.) Plaintiffs have disclosed no expert testimony concerning the relative bioavailability or absorbability of Ester-C and other forms of vitamin C, such as ascorbic acid. (<em>Id.</em> at ¶ 29). <em>Chandler</em> , <extracted-citation case-ids="12526420" index="70" url="https://cite.case.law/f-supp-3d/322/314/#p325"><span class="citation" data-id="7249159"><a href="/opinion/7331241/chandler-v-janssen-pharm-inc/" aria-description="Citation for case: Chandler v. Janssen Pharm., Inc.">322 F.Supp.3d at 325</a></span>-26</extracted-citation>, <extracted-citation index="71" url="https://cite.case.law/citations/?q=2018%20WL%203212422"><span class="citation no-link">2018 WL 3212422</span></extracted-citation>, at *8.</p>
<p id="p-46">Plaintiffs' failure to offer any expert evidence to establish the material falsity or deception element of their false advertising and misrepresentation claims, in itself, compels summary judgment for Defendants on Plaintiffs' false advertising and misrepresentation claims under the UCL, CLRA, FAL, and MMPA.<footnotemark>15</footnotemark></p>
<p id="p-47"><a class="page-label" data-citation-index="1" data-label="874" href="#p874" id="p874">*874</a><strong>IV. Plaintiff Hodjat's UCL Misbranding Claims</strong></p>
<p id="p-48">As discussed <em>supra</em> , Plaintiff Hodjat purports to bring a misbranding claim, based on a "borrowed" predicate violation of California's Sherman Law, under the UCL's "unlawful" practice prong. (Am. Compl. ¶¶ 68-69; <em>see</em> <em>Bruton v. Gerber Food Product Company,</em> <span class="citation" data-id="8683670"><a href="/opinion/8700517/bruton-v-gerber-products-co/#471" aria-description="Citation for case: Bruton v. Gerber Products Co.">703 Fed. App'x 468, 471</a></span> (9th Cir. 2017) (unpublished) (unlawful misbranding claim can be a distinct and separate ground for liability from a fraudulent or false advertising claim under the UCL's unlawfulness prong). However, even when separately asserted, "[t]he Court looks to the 'gravamen' of Plaintiff's [UCL Sherman Law] causes of action to determine if the causes of action are grounded in fraud." <em>Hadley v. Kellogg Sales Co.</em> , <extracted-citation case-ids="12275106" index="72" url="https://cite.case.law/f-supp-3d/243/1074/#p1098"><span class="citation" data-id="7242812"><a href="/opinion/7324900/hadley-v-kellogg-sales-co/" aria-description="Citation for case: Hadley v. Kellogg Sales Co.">243 F.Supp.3d 1074</a></span></extracted-citation>, 1098 (N.D. Cal. 2017) (dismissing unlawful, fraudulent, and unfair misbranding claims under the UCL, where Sherman Law claims were grounded in false advertising, misrepresentation, and fraud) (citing <em>Wilson v. Frito-Lay North America, Inc.,</em> <extracted-citation case-ids="3874657" index="73" url="https://cite.case.law/f-supp-2d/961/1134/#p1144"><span class="citation" data-id="8710402"><a href="/opinion/8727204/wilson-v-frito-lay-north-america-inc/" aria-description="Citation for case: Wilson v. Frito-Lay North America, Inc.">961 F.Supp.2d 1134</a></span></extracted-citation>, 1144 (N.D.Cal. 2013) ("It is clear from Plaintiffs' SAC that the behavior that Plaintiffs allege violated FDA regulations and the Sherman Law is misrepresentation or deception, because Plaintiffs are asserting that Defendant used deceptive labeling practices to hide the truth of the Products' ingredients.") ); <em>Kane v. Chobani, Inc.</em> , No. 12-CV-2425 (LHK), <extracted-citation index="74" url="https://cite.case.law/citations/?q=2013%20WL%205289253"><span class="citation no-link">2013 WL 5289253</span></extracted-citation>, at *6 (N.D. Cal. Sept. 19, 2013) (finding unlawful UCL claim grounded in fraud where the plaintiff alleged a violation of "the UCL's unlawful prong based on Defendant's violations of the advertising and misbranding provisions of the Sherman Law").<footnotemark>16</footnotemark></p>
<p id="p-49">Nowhere is the gravamen of Plaintiff Hodjat's misbranding claims more transparent than in her Amended Complaint: "The Sherman Law provides that a product is misbranded 'if its labeling is false or misleading in any particular.' " (Am. Compl. ¶ 69 (citing <extracted-citation index="75" url="https://cite.case.law/citations/?q=Cal.%20Code%20%C2%A7%20110660"><span class="citation no-link">Cal. Health &amp; Safety Code § 110660</span></extracted-citation> ) ). Here, the clear predicate violations of Plaintiff's UCL misbranding claims are the false or misleading advertising provisions of the Sherman Law. (<em><span class="citation no-link">Id.</span></em> ) Given that Hodjat's UCL misbranding <a class="page-label" data-citation-index="1" data-label="875" href="#p875" id="p875">*875</a>claims are based on the false advertising and misrepresentation provisions of the Sherman Law, they fail for the same reasons as her other claims, <em>i.e.</em> , insufficient evidence to satisfy the reasonable consumer standard or establish the falsity of the implied disease claims alleged. Defendants, therefore, are entitled to summary judgment as to Hodjat's three UCL misbranding claims.</p>
<p id="p-50"><strong>V. Plaintiffs' Law of the Case Argument</strong></p>
<p id="p-51">Plaintiffs try to cure the defects in Plaintiff Hodjat's UCL misbranding claims by arguing that, if she can show that Defendants "violated the [Federal Drug and Cosmetic Act ("FDCA") ] and the parallel California state law, the Sherman Food, Drug, and Cosmetic Act", she need not prove <em>any</em> of the elements of a false advertising/misrepresentation claim, <em>i.e.</em> , that a reasonable consumer perceived the allegedly false implied statements, falsity of the implied statements, causation/reliance, and damages. (Pl. Opp. Br. at ECF 14-16.)<footnotemark>17</footnotemark> From this premise, Hodjat argues that the Court's March 2015 ruling that Plaintiff had created a factual issue about whether the statements on Ester-C's label constituted non-FDA-approved disease claims, <em>Hughes</em> , <extracted-citation case-ids="5923810" index="76" url="https://cite.case.law/f-supp-3d/99/278/"><span class="citation" data-id="7230937"><a href="/opinion/7313032/hughes-v-ester-c-co/" aria-description="Citation for case: Hughes v. Ester C Co.">99 F.Supp.3d at 286</a></span>-87</extracted-citation>, constitutes the "law of the case" and results in Hodjat's UCL claim surviving summary judgment, regardless of whether she can prove the other elements of her claim. (Pl. Opp. Br. at ECF 15 (asserting that "the Court has already rejected Defendants' argument that the representations at issue do not constitute a disease claim").) This circular sleight of hand, however, is incorrect.<footnotemark>18</footnotemark></p>
<p id="p-52">The "law-of-the-case" doctrine stands for the proposition that "a decision regarding an issue of law made at one stage of a litigation becomes binding precedent, to be followed in subsequent stages of the same litigation." <em>Firestone v. Berrios</em> , <extracted-citation case-ids="4344088" index="77" url="https://cite.case.law/f-supp-3d/42/403/#p411"><span class="citation" data-id="7226313"><a href="/opinion/7308410/firestone-v-berrios/" aria-description="Citation for case: Firestone v. Berrios">42 F.Supp.3d 403</a></span></extracted-citation>, 411 (E.D.N.Y. 2013) (internal quotation omitted). However, that doctrine, which is "discretionary", <em>New York City Dep't of Fin. v. Twin Rivers, Inc.</em> , No. 95-CV-1389 (HB) (HBP), <extracted-citation index="78" url="https://cite.case.law/citations/?q=1997%20WL%20299423"><span class="citation no-link">1997 WL 299423</span></extracted-citation>, at *1 (S.D.N.Y. June 5, 1997) (internal quotation omitted), does not apply where there has been an "intervening change of facts (based on the discovery of new evidence) or of controlling law ...." <em>Calabrese v. CSC Holdings, Inc.,</em> No. 02-CV-5171 (DLI)(JO), <extracted-citation index="79" url="https://cite.case.law/citations/?q=2009%20WL%20425879"><span class="citation no-link">2009 WL 425879</span></extracted-citation>, at *7 (E.D.N.Y. Feb. 19, 2009) (citing <em>Official Comm. of the Unsecured Creditors of Color Tile, Inc. v. Coopers &amp; Lybrand, L.L.P.,</em> <extracted-citation case-ids="9368365,9358511" index="80" url="https://cite.case.law/f3d/322/147/"><span class="citation" data-id="781162"><a href="/opinion/781162/official-committee-of-the-unsecured-creditors-of-color-tile-inc-as/" aria-description="Citation for case: Official Committee of the Unsecured Creditors of Color...">322 F.3d 147</a></span></extracted-citation>, 167 (2d Cir. 2003) (internal citations omitted) ). Here, the relevant facts have changed. While the Court found <a class="page-label" data-citation-index="1" data-label="876" href="#p876" id="p876">*876</a>in March 2015, based on the record then, that there existed a material issue of fact about whether Defendants' "immune support" statements constituted implied disease claims, there is no longer a factual dispute on this issue.</p>
<p id="p-53">As discussed <em>supra</em> , based on the now-complete record, Plaintiffs are unable to demonstrate that the Ester-C labels even conveyed the alleged implied disease claims, because Plaintiffs have failed to put forth anything more than their own subjective beliefs about what messages the labels conveyed. <em>See, e.g.,</em> <em>Stone v. Eamer</em> , No. 7:15-CV-97 (LEK) (ATB), <extracted-citation index="81" url="https://cite.case.law/citations/?q=2018%20WL%20557872"><span class="citation no-link">2018 WL 557872</span></extracted-citation>, at *14, n. 8 (N.D.N.Y. Jan. 19, 2018) (granting summary judgment to Defendants, departing from prior order finding that Plaintiff may assert a plausible claim under Title VI of the Civil Rights Act of 1964 ("Title VI"), based on Plaintiff's intervening failure to provide sufficient evidence to establish a "deliberate indifference" Title VI claim). Thus, Hodjat's assertion that "the Court has already rejected Defendants' argument that the representations at issue do not constitute a disease claim" is simply incorrect. Nor can Hodjat even make the conditional argument that, "<em>if</em> a jury determines that Defendants violated federal regulations by making disease claims about Ester-C without prior authorization, causation, reliance and damages are established as a matter of law[ ]". (Pl. Opp. Br. at ECF 16 (emphasis added) ). That option is now foreclosed. Because Plaintiffs have failed to develop any evidence to show that the implied disease claims even exist, Hodjat is unable to show that Defendants violated federal regulations, so as to relieve Hodjat of having to prove all of the elements of her UCL misbranding claims. (<span class="citation no-link"><em>Id.</em> at 9-10</span> (arguing that unauthorized disease claims are material under the UCL and that materiality under the UCL establishes causation, reliance, and damages).)<footnotemark>19</footnotemark> For this reason, and the reasons discussed <em>supra,</em> the Court thus finds Plaintiff Hodjat's UCL misbranding claims cannot survive summary judgment.</p>
<p id="p-54"><strong>VI. Unjust Enrichment under New York Common Law</strong></p>
<p id="p-55">To state a claim for unjust enrichment, Plaintiff "must plead that (1) the defendant was enriched (2) at the plaintiff's expense and (3) that equity and good conscience require the defendant to make restitution." <em>Eidelman v. Sun Prod. Corp.</em> , No. 16-CV-3914 (NSR), <extracted-citation index="82" url="https://cite.case.law/citations/?q=2017%20WL%204277187"><span class="citation no-link">2017 WL 4277187</span></extracted-citation>, at *6 (S.D.N.Y. Sept. 25, 2017) (citing <em>Goldemberg v. Johnson &amp; Johnson Consumer Companies, Inc.</em> , <extracted-citation case-ids="4209020" index="83" url="https://cite.case.law/f-supp-3d/8/467/#p483"><span class="citation" data-id="7223353"><a href="/opinion/7305450/goldemberg-v-johnson-johnson-consumer-companies-inc/" aria-description="Citation for case: Goldemberg v. Johnson &amp; Johnson Consumer Companies, Inc.">8 F.Supp.3d 467</a></span></extracted-citation>, 483 (S.D.N.Y. 2014) ); <em>accord</em> <em>Corsello v. Verizon N.Y., Inc.</em> , <extracted-citation case-ids="5719503" index="84" url="https://cite.case.law/ny3d/18/777/#p790"><span class="citation" data-id="2434749"><a href="/opinion/2434749/corsello-v-verizon-new-york-inc/" aria-description="Citation for case: Corsello v. Verizon New York, Inc.">18 N.Y.3d 777</a></span></extracted-citation>, 790, <extracted-citation case-ids="5719503" index="85" url="https://cite.case.law/ny3d/18/777/#p790"><span class="citation" data-id="2434749"><a href="/opinion/2434749/corsello-v-verizon-new-york-inc/" aria-description="Citation for case: Corsello v. Verizon New York, Inc.">944 N.Y.S.2d 732</a></span></extracted-citation>, <extracted-citation case-ids="5719503" index="86" url="https://cite.case.law/ny3d/18/777/#p790"><span class="citation" data-id="2434749"><a href="/opinion/2434749/corsello-v-verizon-new-york-inc/" aria-description="Citation for case: Corsello v. Verizon New York, Inc.">967 N.E.2d 1177</a></span></extracted-citation> (2012) ("The basis of a claim for unjust enrichment is that the defendant has obtained a benefit which 'in equity and good conscience' should be paid to the plaintiff").</p>
<p id="p-56">"Unjust enrichment is not a catchall cause of action to be used when others fail .... An unjust enrichment claim is not available where it simply duplicates, or replaces, a conventional contract or tort claim." <em>Corsello v. Verizon N.Y., Inc.</em> , <extracted-citation case-ids="5719503" index="87" url="https://cite.case.law/ny3d/18/777/#p790"><span class="citation" data-id="2434749"><a href="/opinion/2434749/corsello-v-verizon-new-york-inc/" aria-description="Citation for case: Corsello v. Verizon New York, Inc.">18 N.Y.3d 777</a></span></extracted-citation>, 790, <extracted-citation case-ids="5719503" index="88" url="https://cite.case.law/ny3d/18/777/#p790"><span class="citation" data-id="2434749"><a href="/opinion/2434749/corsello-v-verizon-new-york-inc/" aria-description="Citation for case: Corsello v. Verizon New York, Inc.">944 N.Y.S.2d 732</a></span></extracted-citation>, <extracted-citation case-ids="5719503" index="89" url="https://cite.case.law/ny3d/18/777/#p790"><span class="citation" data-id="2434749"><a href="/opinion/2434749/corsello-v-verizon-new-york-inc/" aria-description="Citation for case: Corsello v. Verizon New York, Inc.">967 N.E.2d 1177</a></span></extracted-citation> (2012). Rather, it "is available <em>only</em> in unusual situations when, though the defendant has not breached a contract nor committed a recognized tort, circumstances create an equitable obligation running from the defendant to the plaintiff." <em><extracted-citation case-ids="5719503" index="90" url="https://cite.case.law/ny3d/18/777/#p790"><span class="citation" data-id="2434749"><a href="/opinion/2434749/corsello-v-verizon-new-york-inc/" aria-description="Citation for case: Corsello v. Verizon New York, Inc.">Id.</a></span></extracted-citation></em> (emphasis <a class="page-label" data-citation-index="1" data-label="877" href="#p877" id="p877">*877</a>added). Unjust enrichment claims should be dismissed "where the violative conduct alleged is conterminous with a conventional tort or contract claim, regardless of whether the tort or contract claim is dismissed." <em>Obeid on behalf of Gemini Real Estate Advisors LLC v. La Mack</em> , No. 14-CV-6498 (LTS) (HBP), <extracted-citation index="91" url="https://cite.case.law/citations/?q=2018%20WL%202059653"><span class="citation no-link">2018 WL 2059653</span></extracted-citation>, at *29 (S.D.N.Y. May 1, 2018) (citing <em>Trainum v. Rockwell Collins, Inc.</em> , No. 16-CV-7005 (JSR), <extracted-citation index="92" url="https://cite.case.law/citations/?q=2017%20WL%202377988"><span class="citation no-link">2017 WL 2377988</span></extracted-citation>, at *19-20 (S.D.N.Y. May 31, 2017) ).</p>
<p id="p-57">This is not an "unusual" situation involving an "equitable obligation" not otherwise recognized by the law. The causes of action in this case are standard false-advertising claims that are governed by consumer protection statutes. Because Plaintiffs' unjust enrichment claim "simply duplicates, or replaces, [these] conventional contract or tort claim[s]", <em>Corsello,</em> <extracted-citation case-ids="5719503" index="93" url="https://cite.case.law/ny3d/18/777/#p790"><span class="citation" data-id="2434749"><a href="/opinion/2434749/corsello-v-verizon-new-york-inc/" aria-description="Citation for case: Corsello v. Verizon New York, Inc.">18 N.Y.3d at 790</a></span></extracted-citation>, <extracted-citation case-ids="5719503" index="94" url="https://cite.case.law/ny3d/18/777/#p790"><span class="citation" data-id="2434749"><a href="/opinion/2434749/corsello-v-verizon-new-york-inc/" aria-description="Citation for case: Corsello v. Verizon New York, Inc.">944 N.Y.S.2d 732</a></span></extracted-citation>, <extracted-citation case-ids="5719503" index="95" url="https://cite.case.law/ny3d/18/777/#p790"><span class="citation" data-id="2434749"><a href="/opinion/2434749/corsello-v-verizon-new-york-inc/" aria-description="Citation for case: Corsello v. Verizon New York, Inc.">967 N.E.2d 1177</a></span></extracted-citation>, it cannot survive summary judgment. <em>See also</em> <em>Davis v. Hain Celestial Grp., Inc.</em> , <extracted-citation case-ids="12524049" index="96" url="https://cite.case.law/f-supp-3d/297/327/"><span class="citation" data-id="7246978"><a href="/opinion/7329062/davis-v-hain-celestial-grp-inc/" aria-description="Citation for case: Davis v. Hain Celestial Grp., Inc.">297 F.Supp.3d 327</a></span></extracted-citation> (E.D.N.Y. 2018) (dismissing plaintiff's unjust enrichment claim where "it merely duplicate[d] plaintiff[']s other claims based on the same alleged misrepresentations" and was "no different from his claims for false advertising or fraudulent misrepresentation."); <em>Mahoney v. Endo Health Solutions</em> , No. 15-CV-9841 (DLC), <extracted-citation index="97" url="https://cite.case.law/citations/?q=2016%20WL%203951185"><span class="citation no-link">2016 WL 3951185</span></extracted-citation>, at *11 (E.D.N.Y. Jul. 20, 2016) (same).</p>
<p id="p-58"><strong>CONCLUSION</strong></p>
<p id="p-59">For the foregoing reasons, the Court grants Defendants' motion for summary judgment. The Clerk of the Court is respectfully directed to enter judgment and close the case accordingly.</p>
