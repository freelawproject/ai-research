<p id="AjS"><em>(dissenting). I </em>respectfully dissent from the Court’s opinion. Plaintiffs requested an instruction on ordinary negligence which, it appears to me, was applicable under the facts presented at trial. <em>Fogel v Sinai Hospital of Detroit, 2 </em>Mich App 99; <span class="citation" data-id="1724495"><a href="/opinion/1724495/fogel-v-sinai-hospital-of-detroit/" aria-description="Citation for case: Fogel v. Sinai Hospital of Detroit">138 NW2d 503</a></span> (1965); <em>Gold v Sinai Hospital of Detroit, </em><span class="citation" data-id="2224026"><a href="/opinion/2224026/gold-v-sinai-hospital-of-detroit-inc/" aria-description="Citation for case: Gold v. Sinai Hospital of Detroit, Inc.">5 Mich App 368</a></span>; <span class="citation" data-id="2224026"><a href="/opinion/2224026/gold-v-sinai-hospital-of-detroit-inc/" aria-description="Citation for case: Gold v. Sinai Hospital of Detroit, Inc.">146 NW2d 723</a></span> (1966). Although plaintiffs initially proceeded under a malpractice theory, GCR 1963, 118.3 permits the amendment of pleadings to conform to evidence adduced at trial provided that the amendment would not prejudice the opposing party. See, <em>Denno v Providence Hospital, </em><span class="citation" data-id="2181262"><a href="/opinion/2181262/denno-v-providence-hospital/" aria-description="Citation for case: Denno v. Providence Hospital">19 Mich App 547</a></span>; <span class="citation" data-id="2181262"><a href="/opinion/2181262/denno-v-providence-hospital/" aria-description="Citation for case: Denno v. Providence Hospital">172 NW2d 918</a></span> (1969). No prejudice would have occurred by allowing the amendment and the attendant jury instruction on ordinary negligence. Inasmuch as the standard of care applied to a trained professional is more stringent than that applied to a lay person, defense of the malpractice theory would encompass the defense necessary under an ordinary negligence theory. I would have allowed the instruction to be given.</p>
