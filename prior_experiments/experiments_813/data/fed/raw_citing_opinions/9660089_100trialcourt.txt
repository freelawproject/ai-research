                   II.  Legal Standard                                
   A complaint should be dismissed under Rule 12(b)(1) only where the 
court lacks jurisdiction over the subject matter of the dispute. Fed. R. Civ. P. 
12(b)(1). “Because a federal court is powerless to act beyond its statutory grant 
of subject matter jurisdiction, a court must zealously insure that jurisdiction 
exists over a case[.]”         , </pre><span class="citation multiple-matches"><a href="/c/F.3d/236/1292/">236 F.3d 1292</a></span><pre class="inline">, 1299 (11th Cir. 2001).  
                            3                                         
T:\ORDERS\19\Callen\mtdtwt2.docx                                        
Attacks on subject matter jurisdiction come in two forms: “facial attacks” and 
“factual attacks.”                                  , </pre><span class="citation" data-id="733352"><a href="/opinion/733352/luis-e-garcia-md-v-copenhaver-bell-associates-mds-pa/#1260" aria-description="Citation for case: Luis E. Garcia, M.D. v. Copenhaver, Bell &amp; Associates,...">104 F.3d          
1256, 1260</a></span><pre class="inline"> (11th Cir. 1997). Facial attacks on the complaint “require the court 

merely to look and see if the plaintiff has sufficiently alleged a basis of subject 
matter jurisdiction, and the allegations in his complaint are taken as true for 
the purposes of the motion.”   at 1261 (internal quotation marks, citation, 
and punctuation omitted). On a facial attack, therefore, “a plaintiff is afforded 
safeguards similar to those provided in opposing a Rule 12(b)(6) motion[.]” 
              , </pre><span class="citation" data-id="552261"><a href="/opinion/552261/dolcie-lawrence-v-peter-dunbar-united-states-of-america/#1529" aria-description="Citation for case: Dolcie Lawrence v. Peter Dunbar, United States of America">919 F.2d 1525, 1529</a></span><pre class="inline"> (11th Cir. 1990).                 

   A complaint should be dismissed under Rule 12(b)(6) only where it  
appears that the facts alleged fail to state a “plausible” claim for relief.
    , </pre><span class="citation" data-id="9435339"><a href="/opinion/145875/ashcroft-v-iqbal/#1949" aria-description="Citation for case: Ashcroft v. Iqbal">129 S. Ct. 1937, 1949</a></span><pre class="inline"> (2009); Fed. R. Civ. P. 12(b)(6). A complaint may 
survive a motion to dismiss for failure to state a claim even if it is “improbable” 
that a plaintiff would be able to prove those facts; even if the possibility of 
recovery is extremely “remote and unlikely.”            , </pre><span class="citation" data-id="9435068"><a href="/opinion/145730/bell-atlantic-corp-v-twombly/#556" aria-description="Citation for case: Bell Atlantic Corp. v. Twombly">550           
U.S. 544, 556</a></span><pre class="inline"> (2007). In ruling on a motion to dismiss, the court must accept 

the facts pleaded in the complaint as true and construe them in the light most 
favorable to the plaintiff.                                             
                     , </pre><span class="citation" data-id="421226"><a href="/opinion/421226/quality-foods-de-centro-america-sa-and-duroparts-de-el-salvador-sa-v/" aria-description="Citation for case: Quality Foods De Centro America, S.A. And Duroparts De El...">711 F.2d 989</a></span><pre class="inline">, 994–95 (11th Cir. 1983);         
                                         , </pre><span class="citation" data-id="682480"><a href="/opinion/682480/rafael-sanjuan-and-marcello-a-maviglia-v-the-american-board-of-psychiatry/#251" aria-description="Citation for case: Rafael Sanjuan and Marcello A. Maviglia v. The American...">40 F.3d 247, 251</a></span><pre class="inline"> (7th      
Cir. 1994) (noting that at the pleading stage, the plaintiff “receives the benefit 
of imagination”). Generally, notice pleading is all that is required for a valid

complaint.                            , </pre><span class="citation" data-id="447053"><a href="/opinion/447053/lombards-inc-v-prince-manufacturing-inc-and-chesebrough-ponds-inc/#975" aria-description="Citation for case: Lombard&#x27;s, Inc. v. Prince Manufacturing, Inc. And...">753 F.2d 974, 975</a></span><pre class="inline"> (11th Cir.    
1985),         , </pre><span class="citation multiple-matches"><a href="/c/U.S./474/1082/">474 U.S. 1082</a></span><pre class="inline"> (1986). Under notice pleading, the plaintiff

   B. Standing to Represent a National Class                          
   “[I]t is well-settled that prior to the certification of a class . . . the district 
court must determine that at least one named class representative has Article 

III standing to raise each class subclaim.”                 ,           
</pre><span class="citation multiple-matches"><a href="/c/F.3d/221/1266/">221 F.3d 1266</a></span><pre class="inline">, 1279 (11th Cir. 2000). “It is not enough that a named plaintiff 
can establish a case or controversy between himself and the defendant by 
virtue of having standing as to one of many claims he wishes to assert.”   at 
1280. “Rather, each claim must be analyzed separately, and a claim cannot be 
asserted on behalf of a class unless at least one named plaintiff has suffered 

the injury that gives rise to that claim.”  (quotation marks and citation 
omitted). Following  , “named plaintiffs in class actions have, time and 
again, been prohibited from asserting claims under a state law other than that 
which the plaintiff’s own claim arises.”         , No. 17-61150,        
</pre><span class="citation no-link">2018 WL 8300534</span><pre class="inline">, at *6 (S.D. Fla. Mar. 28, 2018). Because standing is a 
threshold issue, courts in this circuit routinely resolve it on a motion to dismiss, 
rather than waiting for class certification.                            

  , </pre><span class="citation no-link">2021 WL 1216897</span><pre class="inline">, at *6 (S.D. Fla. Mar. 30, 2021) (collecting cases).

Defs.’  Mot.  to  Dismiss,  at  6.)  As  explained  above,  the  Court  has  already
