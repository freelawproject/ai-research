<opinion type="majority">
<author id="b370-8">CONERY, Judge.</author>
<p id="b370-9">hAH remaining parties to this litigation have appealed a part of the judgment rendered by the trial court on August 4, 2016. Defendants, Lafayette Consolidated Government and Officer Todd Rogers (LCG Defendants), appeal the denial by the trial court of their peremptory exception of prescription. The plaintiff, Brittni Castille, and third party claimants, the Estate of Trissy Hargrave and Raywood and Ella Hargrave, who during the litigation substituted themselves in place of the Estate of Trissy Hargrave (collectively referred to as the Estate of Trissy Hargrave), appeal the ruling of the trial court after a trial on the merits finding that the LCG Defendants were free from fault, and thereby dismissing all claims against the LCG Defendants made by Brittni Castille and the Estate of Trissy Hargrave at their cost.</p>
<p id="b370-11">For the following reasons, we reverse the trial court’s denial of the LCG Defendants’ peremptory exception of prescription, which renders moot the appeal of Brittni Castille and the Estate of Trissy Hargrave of the trial court’s ruling in favor of the remaining LCG Defendants, Lafayette Consolidated Government and Officer Rogers.</p>
<p id="b370-12">PROCEDURAL HISTORY</p>
<p id="b370-13">The initial appeal of the plaintiff, Brittni Castille, was taken from the trial court’s reason’s for judgment, and docketed under number 16-798. However, subsequent to its granting the plaintiffs order of appeal, the trial court signed a judgment in conformity with its reasons for judgment on August 4, 2016. The Louisiana Supreme Court in <em>Overmier v. Traylor, </em><span class="citation" data-id="1772937"><a href="/opinion/1772937/overmier-v-traylor/#1094" aria-description="Citation for case: Overmier v. Traylor">475 So.2d 1094, 1094-95</a></span> (La. 1985) (emphasis added and footnote omitted), overturned the appellate court’s dismissal of an appeal taken from the trial court’s reasons for judgment and stated, |a“an appeal granted before the signing of a final judgment is subject to dismissal <em>until </em>the final judgment is signed. However, once the final judgment has been signed, any previously existing defect has been cured, and there is no useful purpose in dismissing the otherwise valid appeal.”</p>
<p id="b370-14">The August 4, 2016 judgment was transmitted to this court and docketed under number 16-1049. Although these two cases are not consolidated, they are identical, and this court’s ruling under this docket number will be reflected in docket number 16-1049.</p>

<p id="b373-10">ASSIGNMENTS OF ERROR</p>
<p id="b373-11">The remaining LCG Defendants, Lafayette Consolidated Government and Officer Todd Rogers, assign the following errors on appeal.</p>
<blockquote id="b373-12">1) The trial court [erred] in finding that pre-lawsuit settlement agreements between the plaintiffs on the one hand and the negligent driver on the other was an acknowledgement of liability, interrupting prescription against the LCG Parties.</blockquote>
<blockquote id="b373-13">2) The trial court erred when it first found that an acknowledgment of prescription had occurred and then <page-number citation-index="1" label="342">*342</page-number>imputed it to unrelated LCG Parties.</blockquote>
<blockquote id="b374-3">3) The trial court misapplied Louisiana Civil Code Article 2324 (C) by finding that prescription had been interrupted as to the LCG Parties even though no one could remotely be considered a joint tortfeasor has ever been sued or served.</blockquote>
<p id="b374-4">| <em>Standard of Review—Prescription</em></p>
<p id="acr-dedup-0">In the recent case of <em>Lomont v. Bennett, </em>14-2483, p. 8 (La. 6/30/15), <span class="citation" data-id="2813277"><a href="/opinion/2813277/tracy-ray-lomont-v-michelle-myer-bennett-and-xyz-insurance-company/#627" aria-description="Citation for case: Tracy Ray Lomont v. Michelle Myer-Bennett and Xyz...">172 So.3d 620, 627</a></span>, <em>cert. denied, </em>— U.S. —, <span class="citation multiple-matches"><a href="/c/S.Ct./136/1167/">136 S.Ct. 1167</a></span>, <span class="citation multiple-matches"><a href="/c/L.Ed.2d/194/178/">194 L.Ed.2d 178</a></span> (2016) (citations omitted), our supreme court clarified the appellate standard of review applicable to a peremptory exception as follows:</p>
<blockquote id="b374-7">At a hearing on a peremptory exception of prescription pleaded prior to trial, evidence may be introduced to support or controvert the exception. In the absence of evidence, an exception of per-emption must be decided upon the facts alleged in the petition with all of the allegations accepted as true. However, when evidence is introduced, the court is not bound to accept plaintiffs allegations as true. If evidence is introduced at the hearing on the peremptory exception of peremption, the district court’s findings of fact are reviewed under the manifest error-clearly wrong standard of review. If those findings are reasonable in light of the record reviewed in its entirety, an appellate court cannot reverse even though convinced that had it been sitting as a trier of fact, it would have weighed the evidence differently.</blockquote>
<p id="b374-8">The peremptory exception of prescription urged by the LCG Defendants was filed after the plaintiff and third party claimants had rested their case, and prior to submission of the matter to the trial court for decision. Therefore, the LCG Defendants’ peremptory exception of prescription is timely pursuant to La.Code Civ.P, art. 928, which provides in pertinent part, “[t]he peremptory exception of prescription may be pleaded at any stage of the proceeding in the trial court prior to the submission of the case for decision.”</p>
<p id="b374-10">Further, as evidence was introduced at the trial relevant to the peremptory exception of prescription, we must review the entire record to determine if the trial court manifestly erred in making its factual conclusions.</p>
<p id="b374-11">LAW AND DISCUSSION</p>
<p id="b374-12">Louisiana Civil Code Article 3429 governs the prescriptive period for a delictual action and provides in pertinent part, “[djelictual actions are subject to a | Jiberative prescription of one year. This prescription commences to run from the day injury or damage is sustained.” Louisiana Civil Code Article 2324(A) requires that, “[h]e who conspires with another person to commit an intentional or willful act is answerable, in solido, with that person, for the damage caused by such act.” Likewise, La.Civ.Code art. 2324(B) states in pertinent part, “[i]f the liability is not soli-dary pursuant to Paragraph A, then liability for damages caused by two or more persons shall be a joint and divisible obligation.” However, La.Civ.Code art. 2324(C) provides that “[ijnterruption of prescription against one joint tortfeasor is effective against all joint tortfeasors.”</p>
<p id="b374-13">In the recent case of <em>Correro v. Ferrer, </em>16-861, p. 4-5 (La. 10/28/16), — So.3d —, —, our supreme court recently reiterated the law pertaining to prescription and stated:</p>
<blockquote id="b374-16">As we have long held, prescriptive statutes are strictly construed in favor of maintaining a plaintiffs’ cause of action. <em>Williams v. Jackson Parish Hospital, </em>00-3170, p. 13 (La. 10/16/01), <span class="citation multiple-matches"><a href="/c/So.2d/798/921/">798 So.2d 921</a></span>, 930. Absent clear, contrary <page-number citation-index="1" label="343">*343</page-number>legislative intent, “prescriptive statutes which can be given more than one reasonable interpretation should be construed against the party claiming prescription.” <em>Maltby v. Gauthier, </em><span class="citation" data-id="1740668"><a href="/opinion/1740668/maltby-v-gauthier/" aria-description="Citation for case: Maltby v. Gauthier">506 So.2d 1190</a></span>,1193 n. 5 (La. 1987). Thus, if there are two possible constructions, the one which favors maintaining an action, as opposed to barring, should be adopted. <em>Carter v. Haygood, </em>04-0646, p. 10 (La. 1/19/05), <span class="citation" data-id="1110876"><a href="/opinion/1110876/carter-v-haygood/#1268" aria-description="Citation for case: Carter v. Haygood">892 So.2d 1261, 1268</a></span>.</blockquote>
<p id="AtJH">The Louisiana Supreme Court in <em>Prevo v. State ex rel Department of Public Safety and Corrections Division of Probation and Parole, </em>15-823 (La. 11/20/15), <span class="citation" data-id="4826002"><a href="/opinion/5013363/prevo-v-state-ex-rel-department-of-public-safety-corrections-division/#398" aria-description="Citation for case: Prevo v. State ex rel. Department of Public Safety &amp;...">187 So.3d 395, 398</a></span>, reiterated the burden of proof to be applied in determining a peremptory exception of prescription:</p>
<blockquote id="ahc-dedup-0">Ordinarily, the exceptor bears the burden of proof at the trial of the peremptory exception. <em>Campo v. Correa, </em>01-2707, p. 7 (La. 6/21/02), <span class="citation" data-id="1132521"><a href="/opinion/1132521/campo-v-correa/#508" aria-description="Citation for case: Campo v. Correa">828 So.2d 502, 508</a></span>. However, if prescription is evident on the face of |inthe pleadings, the burden shifts to the plaintiff to show the action has not prescribed. <em><span class="citation" data-id="1132521"><a href="/opinion/1132521/campo-v-correa/" aria-description="Citation for case: Campo v. Correa">Campo</a></span>, </em>01-2707 at p. 7, <span class="citation" data-id="1132521"><a href="/opinion/1132521/campo-v-correa/#508" aria-description="Citation for case: Campo v. Correa">828 So.2d at 508</a></span>; <em>Williams v. Sewerage &amp; Water Bd. of New Orleans, </em><span class="citation" data-id="1924997"><a href="/opinion/1924997/williams-v-sewerage-water-bd-of-no/#1386" aria-description="Citation for case: Williams v. Sewerage &amp; Water Bd. of NO">611 So.2d 1383,1386</a></span> (La.1993).</blockquote>
<p id="b375-5">As previously stated, Brittni Castille sued the LCG Defendants on January 28, 2011, some thirteen months after the December 4, 2009 accident. Additionally, third party plaintiff, the Estate of Trissy Hargrave sued the LCG Defendants on February 15, 2011, more than fourteen months after the December 14, 2009 accident. Therefore the burden is on plaintiff, Brittni Castille, and the third party plaintiff, the Estate of Trissy Hargrave, to show that prescription has been interrupted based on La.Civ.Code art. 2324(C).</p>
<p id="b375-6">THE TRIAL COURT’S REASONS FOR RULING</p>
<p id="b375-7">The trial court found that prescription had been interrupted and denied the remaining LCG Defendants’ peremptory exception of prescription, stating the following as its basis for ruling (emphasis added):</p>
<blockquote id="b375-9">EXCEPTIONS OF PRESCRIPTION FILED BY LAFAYETTE CITY-PARISH CONSOLIDATED GOVERNMENT AND OFFICER TODD ROGERS</blockquote>
<blockquote id="b375-10">Exceptors argued that prescription had run due to the lack of fault on the part of Trissy Hargrave, thus she was not a joint or solidary obligor such that an interruption of prescription would have occurred. The. accident occurred on December 4, 2009. USAA, Andrew Garner’s insurer acknowledged liability and interrupted prescription as late as May 7, 2010. Interruption of prescription against one joint tortfeasor interrupts prescription against all joint tortfeasors. La.[Civ.Code art.] 2324. <em>[Mallett] v. McNeal, </em>[5-2289, 5-2322], p. 15 [ (La. 10/17/06) ], <span class="citation" data-id="1614438"><a href="/opinion/1614438/mallett-v-mcneal/#1264" aria-description="Citation for case: Mallett v. McNeal">939 So.2d 1254, 1264</a></span>, holds that the unconditional payment of property damage interrupts prescription. Property damage was paid, and deposition of Debra Walter establishes that USAA issued a check to reimburse Farm Bureau for the payment of property damage in 2010.</blockquote>
<blockquote id="b375-11">The Third Party Demand/Cross-Claim by Trissy Hargrave’s parents included a cause of action against Andrew Garner when Pilot’s Pub was sued. The City of Lafayette and Officer Todd Rogers were added Inas defendants prior to the release of Pilot pub at the trial on the merits of this case. Since a cause of action is for negligence, the negligence of co-tortfeasor, Andrew Garner, was pled, the timely filing of the case against Pilot’s Pub, alleging the negligence of Andrew Garner interrupts prescription as to the co-tortfeasors, City of Lafay<page-number citation-index="1" label="344">*344</page-number>ette and Officer Rogers. The City was added as a defendant on February 9, 2011, well within one year of the ac-knowledgement by USAA, Andrew Garner’s insurer. Therefore, LCG’s Exception of Prescription is denied.</blockquote>
<p id="b376-4">
<em>Acknowledgement of Liability by Andrew Garner’s Insurer USAA</em>
</p>
<p id="b376-5">Louisiana Civil Code Article 3464 provides, “[prescription is interrupted when one acknowledges the right of the person against whom he had commenced to prescribe.” The acknowledgement referenced in the trial court’s reasons for judgment was actually contained in the two documents entitled “RELEASE OF All CLAIMS[,]” previously cited in the facts portion of this opinion. ' ■ ,</p>
<p id="b376-6">Contrary to the trial court’s finding of fact, neither document contained the acknowledgment of liability necessary to interrupt prescription. The Release was signed on April 12, 2010, between Brittni L. Castille, as plaintiff, and Catherine D. Garner, The Estate of Andrew M. Gamer, and USAA Casualty Insurance Company. The second Release was signed on May 18, 2010, between Ella Hargrave and Raymond Hargrave, parents of Trissy Har-grave, and Catherine D. Garner, Richard M. Garner, Andrew M. Garner, and USAA-CIC. Both documents contained the following language which unequivocally does not acknowledge any liability on the part of any of the potentially liable parties to the release: “IT BEING FURTHER AGREED AND UNDERSTOOD that this settlement is a compromise of a disputed claim and that the payment is not to be construed as an admission on the part of the party or parties hereby released of any liability whatever in consequence of said accident.” (Emphasis added.)</p>
<p id="b376-9">| t {.Therefore, the trial court erred in finding USAA, Andrew Garner’s insurer, “had acknowledged liability and interrupted prescription as late as May 7, 2010.” Thus, the Release on behalf of the Estate of Trissy Hargrave, signed by her parents, could not serve as an acknowledgment that interrupted prescription as against the remaining LCG Defendants.</p>
<p id="b376-11">The trial court further found, based on the supreme court case of <em>Mallett v. NcNeal, </em>05-2289 (La. 10/17/06), <span class="citation" data-id="1614438"><a href="/opinion/1614438/mallett-v-mcneal/" aria-description="Citation for case: Mallett v. McNeal">939 So.2d 1254</a></span>, that “the unconditional payment of property damages interrupts prescription.” In <em><span class="citation" data-id="1614438"><a href="/opinion/1614438/mallett-v-mcneal/" aria-description="Citation for case: Mallett v. McNeal">Mallett</a></span>, </em>the supreme court actually stated, “an unconditional payment constitutes an acknowledgement sufficient to interrupt prescription.” <em>Mallett, </em><span class="citation" data-id="1614438"><a href="/opinion/1614438/mallett-v-mcneal/#1259" aria-description="Citation for case: Mallett v. McNeal">939 So.2d at 1259</a></span> (emphasis added). As previously stated, the Releases signed by the parties were not “unconditional.”</p>
<p id="b376-12">The trial court also found that an ac-knowledgement sufficient to interrupt prescription occurred when USAA issued a check in 2010 to Farm Bureau for the property damage to the Hargraves’ vehicle, as evidenced by the deposition of Debra Walter, an employee of USAA. However, the trial court failed to state that the supreme court in <em><span class="citation" data-id="1614438"><a href="/opinion/1614438/mallett-v-mcneal/" aria-description="Citation for case: Mallett v. McNeal">Mallett</a></span> </em>proceeded, after defining an “acknowledgment,” to resolve the split in the circuit courts over the application of La.R.S. 22:661, which was renumbered to La.R.S. 22:1290, effective January 1, 2009.</p>
<p id="aad-dedup-1">Louisiana Revised Statute 22:1290 provides:</p>
<blockquote id="b376-14">No settlement made under a vehicle liability insurance policy for a claim against any insured thereunder arising from any accident or other event insured against for damage to or destruction of property owned by another person shall be construed as an admission of liability by the insured, or the insurer’s recognition of such liability, with respect to any other claim arising from the same accident or event.</blockquote>
<p id="b377-4"><page-number citation-index="1" label="345">*345</page-number>1 isThe supreme court in <em><span class="citation" data-id="1614438"><a href="/opinion/1614438/mallett-v-mcneal/" aria-description="Citation for case: Mallett v. McNeal">Mallett</a></span> </em>then agreed with this circuit and held, “that term ‘settlement’ in La.R.S. 22:661[<footnotemark>3</footnotemark>] can be equated with compromise, as the term is defined in La. C.C. art. 3071.”<footnotemark>4</footnotemark> <em>Mallett, </em><span class="citation" data-id="1614438"><a href="/opinion/1614438/mallett-v-mcneal/#1264" aria-description="Citation for case: Mallett v. McNeal">939 So.2d at 1264</a></span>. <em>Cawley v. National Fire &amp; Marine Insurance Co., </em>10-2095, p. 5 (La.App. 1 Cir. 5/6/11), <span class="citation" data-id="4866192"><a href="/opinion/5051800/cawley-v-national-fire-marine-insurance-co/#238" aria-description="Citation for case: Cawley v. National Fire &amp; Marine Insurance Co.">65 So.3d 235, 238</a></span>, also found, based on the holding in <em><span class="citation" data-id="1614438"><a href="/opinion/1614438/mallett-v-mcneal/" aria-description="Citation for case: Mallett v. McNeal">Mallett</a></span>, </em>that “[a] settlement is defined as a compromise and is distinguished from an unconditional payment.”</p>
<p id="b377-5">As in <em><span class="citation" data-id="4866192"><a href="/opinion/5051800/cawley-v-national-fire-marine-insurance-co/" aria-description="Citation for case: Cawley v. National Fire &amp; Marine Insurance Co.">Cawley</a></span>, </em>the application of La.R.S. 22:1290 to the Release signed by the Har-graves on behalf of the Estate of Trissy Hargrave clearly constituted a “settlement.” Thus any payment by USAA to Farm Bureau pursuant to the terms of the Release, “cannot be construed as an admission of liability by the insured, or the insurer’s recognition of such liability, with respect to any other claim arising from the same accident.” La.R.S. 22:1290.</p>
<p id="b377-6">The trial court further found that prescription had been interrupted based on a finding that “[t]he Third-Party Demand/Cross Claim by Trissy Hargrave’s parents included a cause of action against Andrew Garner when Pilot’s Pub was sued.” Pilots’s was timely sued on June 14, 2010. Although the negligence of Andrew Garner was alleged to be the cause of the death and injury, neither Garner nor his insurer, USAA, was named as a defendant in the third party, cross claim of the Estate of Trissy Hargrave. Clearly this was due to the settlement perfected between the Estate of Trissy Hargrave and Andrew Garner and his insurer, USAA, on May 18, 2010, which extinguished any cause of action that might have been brought against Andrew Garner and USAA. Accordingly, the mere allegations of 1 unegligence made against Andrew Garner cannot serve to interrupt prescription against the remaining tortfeasors, Lafayette Consolidated Government and Officer Rogers.</p>
<p id="b377-12">At the time that the remaining LCG defendants, Lafayette Consolidated Government and Officer Rogers, urged their peremptory exception of prescription, no remaining co-defendants who had been timely sued remained as defendants in the case. Pilot’s had been dismissed on summary judgment prior to trial, and Trissy Hargrave had been dismissed pursuant to an involuntary dismissal granted after the plaintiff and third party claimants rested their case.</p>
<p id="b377-13">The Louisiana Supreme Court in <em>Renfroe v. State ex rel. Department of Transportation and Development, </em>01-1646, p. 4 (La. 2/26/02), <span class="citation multiple-matches"><a href="/c/So.2d/809/947/">809 So.2d 947</a></span>, 950, clearly stated that the “interruption of prescription by suit against one solidary obligor is effective as to all solidary obligors. ... The same principle is applicable to joint tortfeasors[,]” under La.Civ.Code art 2324(C), The <em>Renfroe </em>court further stated:</p>
<blockquote id="ada-dedup-1">However, a suit timely filed against one defendant does not interrupt prescription as against any other defendants not timely sued, where the timely sued defendant is ultimately found not liable to the plaintiffs, since no joint or solidary obligation would exist. <em>Spott v. Otis Elevator Co., </em><span class="citation" data-id="1776456"><a href="/opinion/1776456/spott-v-otis-elevator-co/" aria-description="Citation for case: Spott v. Otis Elevator Co.">601 So.2d 1355</a></span> (La.1992).</blockquote>
<p id="a9s-dedup-0"><em>Renfroe, </em>809 So.2d at 950.</p>
<p id="b377-14">For the forgoing reasons, we find, after the required review of the entire record, that the trial court manifestly erred in making its factual conclusions, <page-number citation-index="1" label="346">*346</page-number>and hereby reverse the portion of the trial court’s August 4, 2016 judgment denying Lafayette Consolidated Government and Officer Rogers’ peremptory exception of prescription.</p>
<p id="b378-4">h ¡¿Based on this court’s ruling on the prescription issue, the appeal of Brittni Castille and the Estate of Trissy Hargrave of the second portion of the trial court’s August 4, 2016 judgment in favor of Lafayette Consolidated Government and Officer Rogers dismissing all claims against them is rendered moot.</p>
<p id="b378-5">CONCLUSION</p>
<p id="b378-6">For the foregoing reasons, the trial court’s judgment dated August 4, 2016, denying Lafayette Consolidated Government and Officer Todd Rogers’ peremptory exception of prescription is reversed, thereby dismissing all remaining claims against them made by, plaintiff, Brittni Castille, and third party plaintiff, the Estate of Trissy Hargrave. The appeal of Brittni Castille and the Estate of Trissy Hargrave of the second portion of the trial court’s August 4, 2016 judgment in favor of Lafayette Consolidated Government and Officer Todd Rogers dismissing all claims made against them in this case is rendered moot. All costs of this appeal are assessed equally to Brittni Castille and the Estate of Trissy Hargrave.</p>
<p id="b378-7">REVERSED IN PART AND RENDERED MOOT IN PART.</p>
