<opinion type="majority">
<author id="b468-9">M. J. Kelly, J.</author>
<p id="AYs">On September 13, 1973, Thomas Bushman and Kay Bushman filed a complaint against Burns Clinic Medical Center, P. C., and John Hall, M. D., for damages for medical malpractice and breach of warranty. On December 3, 1973, the Emmet County Circuit Court granted defendant’s motion for an accelerated judgment based upon the expiration of the statute of limitations. MCL 600.5805, 600.5838; MSA 27A.5805, 27A.5838. This Court reversed that decision, <em>Bushman v Burns Clinic Medical Center, P. C., </em>(Docket No. 19333, decided December 4, 1974 [unreported]), and remanded the case for trial.</p>
<p id="b468-10">Plaintiffs were represented by new counsel at trial. In his opening statement to the jury, plaintiffs’ counsel informed the court that plaintiffs were limiting their cause of action to money damages for mental distress,<footnotemark>1</footnotemark> anxiety, discomfort, and <page-number citation-index="1" label="457">*457</page-number>the accompanying physical, mental, and financial effects caused by an unwanted pregnancy. He remarked that those claims were included in the original five-count complaint. During trial plaintiffs did not introduce any proofs on a number of other damage allegations found in the original complaint, but proceeded with a claim narrowed to the issue of damages incurred for wrongful pregnancy as distinguished from wrongful birth.</p>
<p id="b469-4">On February 4, 1977, judgment of no cause for action in favor of defendants was entered upon a January 26, 1977, jury verdict. Plaintiffs appeal of right.</p>
<p id="b469-5">This case involves a vasectomy performed on plaintiff husband by defendant doctor. The vasectomy was ineffective since plaintiff wife later became pregnant with their fifth child. The child was and is in good health.</p>
<p id="A__z">The particular factual situation presents us with what appears to be a case of first impression. One central issue is controlling and dispositive of this appeal, requiring reversal and remand for a new trial because of erroneous instructions and arguments to the jury. The defense theory approved by the trial court was that any damages incurred by the plaintiffs should be offset by the benefits received from having the blessing of a healthy child.</p>
<p id="b469-7">The trial court relied on <em>Troppi v Scarf, </em><span class="citation" data-id="2185063"><a href="/opinion/2185063/troppi-v-scarf/" aria-description="Citation for case: Troppi v. Scarf">31 Mich App 240</a></span>; <span class="citation" data-id="2185063"><a href="/opinion/2185063/troppi-v-scarf/" aria-description="Citation for case: Troppi v. Scarf">187 NW2d 511</a></span> (1971), <em>lv den, </em><span class="citation multiple-matches"><a href="/c/Mich/385/753/">385 Mich 753</a></span> (1971). But reliance on <em>Troppi </em>is misplaced. Aside from damages incurred in a wrongful pregnancy, the plaintiffs in <em>Troppi </em>were seeking the economic costs of rearing their eighth child. The child was conceived after the mother had taken a <page-number citation-index="1" label="458">*458</page-number>drug, provided to her by a pharmacist, represented to be a contraceptive. It actually was a mild tranquilizer.</p>
<p id="b470-6">Plaintiffs in the present case abandoned damage allegations for the economic cost of raising their child, prior to the commencement of trial. We are aware that the <em>Troppi </em>Court did conclude that damages incurred during pregnancy should not be separated from damages for the economic costs of rearing a child. The Court stated:</p>
<blockquote id="b470-7">"Since pregnancy and its attendant anxiety, incapacity, pain, and suffering are inextricably related to child bearing, we do not think it would be sound to attempt to separate those segments of damage from the economic costs of an unplanned child in applying the 'same interest’ rule. Accordingly, the benefits of the unplanned child may be weighed against all the elements of claimed damage.” <span class="citation" data-id="2185063"><a href="/opinion/2185063/troppi-v-scarf/#255" aria-description="Citation for case: Troppi v. Scarf">31 Mich App at 255</a></span>.</blockquote>
<p id="b470-8">The following discussion of case law and public policy leads us to the conclusion that the "benefits rule” set forth in <em>Troppi </em>should not apply to an action narrowly confined to damages for wrongful pregnancy and not wrongful life. To the extent this is inconsistent with <em>Troppi, </em>we stand in disagreement.</p>
<p id="b470-9">Case law in the various jurisdictions is divided on whether or not to allow recovery for wrongful life and make a physician liable for an unplanned child because of his negligent sterilization treatment. A recent New York case noted: "Causes of action for 'wrongful life’ have consistently met with judicial disapproval not only in New York State but in other jurisdictions as well”. <em>Karlsons v Guerinot, </em>57 App Div 2d 73, 79; <span class="citation" data-id="5819188"><a href="/opinion/5960312/karlsons-v-guerinot/#937" aria-description="Citation for case: Karlsons v. Guerinot">394 NYS2d 933, 937</a></span> (1977). See <em>Clegg v Chase, </em><span class="citation" data-id="6197887"><a href="/opinion/6329333/clegg-v-chase/" aria-description="Citation for case: Clegg v. Chase">89 Misc 2d 510</a></span>; <span class="citation" data-id="6197887"><a href="/opinion/6329333/clegg-v-chase/" aria-description="Citation for case: Clegg v. Chase">391 NYS2d 966</a></span> (1977), <em>Terrell v Garcia, </em><span class="citation" data-id="9681810"><a href="/opinion/1778846/terrell-v-garcia/" aria-description="Citation for case: Terrell v. Garcia">496 SW2d 124</a></span> <page-number citation-index="1" label="459">*459</page-number>(Tex Civ App, 1973), <em>cert den </em><span class="citation multiple-matches"><a href="/c/US/415/927/">415 US 927</a></span>; <span class="citation multiple-matches"><a href="/c/S%20Ct/94/1434/">94 S Ct 1434</a></span>; <span class="citation no-link">39 L Ed 2d 484</span> (1974), <em>criticizing Troppi v <span class="citation" data-id="2185063"><a href="/opinion/2185063/troppi-v-scarf/" aria-description="Citation for case: Troppi v. Scarf">Scarf, supra.</a></span></em></p>
<p id="b471-4">The <em><span class="citation" data-id="6197887"><a href="/opinion/6329333/clegg-v-chase/" aria-description="Citation for case: Clegg v. Chase">Clegg</a></span> </em>court did note that "there are many well reasoned authorities to the contrary” allowing damages for wrongful life. <em>Citing Martineau v Nelson, </em><span class="citation" data-id="2131325"><a href="/opinion/2131325/martineau-v-nelson/" aria-description="Citation for case: Martineau v. Nelson">247 NW2d 409</a></span> (Minn, 1976), <em>Custodio v Bauer, </em>251 Cal App 2d 303; 59 Cal Rptr 463; 27 ALR2d 884 (1967). See also <em>Troppi v <span class="citation" data-id="2185063"><a href="/opinion/2185063/troppi-v-scarf/" aria-description="Citation for case: Troppi v. Scarf">Scarf, supra,</a></span> Terrell v Garcia, supra, </em>at 128 (Cadena, J., dissenting), <em>Hays v Hall, </em><span class="citation" data-id="2401296"><a href="/opinion/2401296/hays-v-hall/" aria-description="Citation for case: Hays v. Hall">477 SW2d 402</a></span> (Tex Civ App, 1972), <em>rev’d on other grounds, </em><span class="citation" data-id="2386289"><a href="/opinion/2386289/hays-v-hall/" aria-description="Citation for case: Hays v. Hall">488 SW2d 412</a></span> (Tex, 1972). See generally Anno: <em>Medical Malpractice, and Measure and Element of Damages, in Connection with Sterilization or Birth Control Procedures, </em>27 ALR3d 906, Anno: <em>Tort Liability for Wrongfully Causing One to be Born, </em>22 ALR3d 1441. Various jurisdictions have denied recovery in a wrongful life action when a normal healthy child has been delivered without harm to the mother on the grounds that the benefits of raising a child make the granting of any damages contrary to public policy. 27 ALR3d at 916. Michigan has determined that there is no public policy reason to deny recovery, but has limited recovery by offsetting any benefits received. <em>Troppi v <span class="citation" data-id="2185063"><a href="/opinion/2185063/troppi-v-scarf/" aria-description="Citation for case: Troppi v. Scarf">Scarf, supra.</a></span></em></p>
<p id="b471-5">Plaintiffs in the instant case claim to have been damaged by the tortious conduct of the defendant doctor in performing a vasectomy negligently. Since the plaintiffs have narrowed their claim of damages to wrongful pregnancy,<footnotemark>2</footnotemark> they have <page-number citation-index="1" label="460">*460</page-number>framed an action wherein the policy reasons for denying recovery and offsetting any benefits are far less convincing than in the cases denying recovery for wrongful life.</p>
<p id="b472-6">In <em>Coleman v Garrison, </em><span class="citation" data-id="1538699"><a href="/opinion/1538699/coleman-v-garrison/" aria-description="Citation for case: Coleman v. Garrison">327 A2d 757</a></span> (Del Super, 1974), <em>aff'd </em><span class="citation" data-id="1962630"><a href="/opinion/1962630/coleman-v-garrison/" aria-description="Citation for case: Coleman v. Garrison">349 A2d 8</a></span> (Del, 1975), a Delaware court held that there should be no cause of action for damages for wrongful life, but nevertheless found sufficient reason to allow a cause of action for wrongful pregnancy. The court stated:</p>
<blockquote id="b472-7">"It is the view of this Court that there should be no cause of action so established as to allow damages for 'wrongful life.’ The preciousness of human life should not be held to vary with the circumstances surrounding birth. To make such a determination would, indeed, raise the unfortunate prospect of ruling, as a matter of law, that under certain circumstances a child would not be worth the trouble and expense necessary to bring him into the world. It is not difficult to understand the reluctance of many distinguished jurists to find that the birth of a child is an injury for which plaintiff should deserve an award of damages.</blockquote>
<blockquote id="b472-8">"That is not to say, however, that the plaintiff has not suffered a recognizable injury where avoidable pregnancy has resulted from faulty medical procedure. To the contrary, a ruling that no recognizable cause of action could exist under such circumstances would leave the medical profession virtually immune from liability for improper treatment of patients justifiable <em>[sic] </em>seeking to avoid pregnancy.</blockquote>
<blockquote id="b472-9">"A more appropriate resolution of the difficulties presented, and the one hereby adopted, is to view the action as one for 'wrongful pregnancy’ rather than one for 'wrongful life’ thereby limiting the scope of the injury to the very real expenses, and obvious difficulties attending the unexpected pregnancy of a woman.</blockquote>
<blockquote id="b472-10">"Limitation of the cause of action in such a manner is valid since to do otherwise, would be to invite unduly <page-number citation-index="1" label="461">*461</page-number>speculative and ethically questionable assessments of such matters as the emotional affect of a birth on siblings as well as parents, and the emotional as well as pecuniary costs of raising an unplanned and, perhaps, an unwanted child in varying family environments. Speculative damages are, quite simply, not recoverable in this State. <em>See Laskowski v Wallis, </em><span class="citation" data-id="2384518"><a href="/opinion/2384518/laskowski-v-wallis/" aria-description="Citation for case: Laskowski v. Wallis">205 A.2d 825</a></span> (Del. Supr. 1964); <em>Henne v Balick, </em><span class="citation" data-id="2385113"><a href="/opinion/2385113/henne-v-balick/" aria-description="Citation for case: Henne v. Balick">1 Storey 369</a></span>, <span class="citation" data-id="2385113"><a href="/opinion/2385113/henne-v-balick/" aria-description="Citation for case: Henne v. Balick">146 A.2d 394</a></span> (Del. Supr. 1958).” <span class="citation" data-id="1538699"><a href="/opinion/1538699/coleman-v-garrison/#761" aria-description="Citation for case: Coleman v. Garrison">327 A2d at 761</a></span>.</blockquote>
<p id="b473-4">In Michigan, while we recognize an action to allow damages for "wrongful life” offset by the benefits received by the plaintiffs from that life, the question is: should those benefits offset the potential damages for "wrongful pregnancy”? The <em><span class="citation" data-id="1538699"><a href="/opinion/1538699/coleman-v-garrison/" aria-description="Citation for case: Coleman v. Garrison">Coleman</a></span> </em>case, although not binding on this Court, does suggest that we should treat damages for "wrongful pregnancy” differently than for "wrongful life”. We do not feel constrained by the <em>Troppi </em>Court to follow the "benefits rule” under the instant facts since <em>Troppi </em>was primarily concerned with damages sought for "wrongful life”. We adopt the following findings by the <em><span class="citation" data-id="1538699"><a href="/opinion/1538699/coleman-v-garrison/" aria-description="Citation for case: Coleman v. Garrison">Coleman</a></span> </em>court as applicable in the present case:</p>
<blockquote id="b473-5">"In summary, this Court finds that the plaintiffs may be allowed provable damages only for the following:</blockquote>
<blockquote id="b473-6">1. The pain, suffering and discomfort of [plaintiff wife] as a result of her last pregnancy; and</blockquote>
<blockquote id="b473-7">2. The cost of a [vasectomy]; and</blockquote>
<blockquote id="b473-8">3. The loss to [plaintiff husband] of the comfort, companionship, services and consortium of [plaintiff wife]; however, the loss of consortium is limited to the loss arising from pregnancy and immediately after birth; and</blockquote>
<blockquote id="b473-9">4. The medical expenses incurred by [plaintiffs] as a result of the * * * pregnancy.” <span class="citation" data-id="1538699"><a href="/opinion/1538699/coleman-v-garrison/#761" aria-description="Citation for case: Coleman v. Garrison">327 A2d at 761-762</a></span>.</blockquote>
<p id="b473-10">Further support can be found in <em>Custodio v Bauer, supra, </em>a California case wherein plaintiffs <page-number citation-index="1" label="462">*462</page-number>sought to recover damages resulting from the pregnancy of the plaintiff wife following the failure of a sterilization operation. In reversing a dismissal entered against the plaintiff, the <em>Custodio </em>court held in part that if the plaintiffs established a violation of duty by the defendants they should be awarded damages for the expenses incurred in undergoing the operation. Furthermore, if the plaintiffs could show "physical complications and mental, physical and nervous pain and suffering which the operation was designed to prevent, they should be able to recover”. The court noted that "mental suffering attendant to the unexpected pregnancy because of the complications which may or may not result, the complications that do result, and the delivery of a child are all foreseeable consequences of the failure of the operation”.</p>
<p id="b474-6">In <em>West v Underwood, </em>132 NJL 325; <span class="citation" data-id="3560069"><a href="/opinion/3579599/west-v-underwood/" aria-description="Citation for case: West v. Underwood">40 A2d 610</a></span> (1945), plaintiffs brought an action against defendants for negligently failing to sterilize the plaintiff wife at the time of performing a Caesarean operation. The court noted that there were proofs supporting the allegations. The court ruled that if this were so "[pjlaintiffs were entitled to recover for all pain and suffering, mental and physical, together with loss of services and 'any other loss or damage proximately resulting from such negligence’ ”. See also <em>Betancourt v Gaylor, </em>136 NJ Super 69, 75; <span class="citation" data-id="1930904"><a href="/opinion/1930904/betancourt-v-gaylor/#339" aria-description="Citation for case: Betancourt v. Gaylor">344 A2d 336, 339</a></span> (1975), <em>Anonymous v Hospital, </em>33 Conn Supp 126; <span class="citation no-link">366 A2d 204</span> (1976).</p>
<p id="b474-7">A jury could not be expected to place the value of a child’s life to his parents as less than damages incurred during an unwanted pregnancy. To expand the scope of <em>Troppi </em>to the instant facts would, in effect, outlaw a cause of action for wrongful pregnancy. Again we quote from <em>Coleman, supra:</em></p>
<blockquote id="b475-3"><page-number citation-index="1" label="463">*463</page-number>"[A] ruling that no recognizable cause of action could exist under such circumstances would leave the medical profession virtually immune from liability for improper treatment of patients justifiable <em>[sic] </em>seeking to avoid pregnancy.” <span class="citation" data-id="1538699"><a href="/opinion/1538699/coleman-v-garrison/#761" aria-description="Citation for case: Coleman v. Garrison">327 A2d at 761</a></span>.</blockquote>
<p id="b475-4">Damages may be established without speculation and conjecture. <em>Cf. Coleman v Garrison, supra, </em>at 761, <span class="citation" data-id="9681810"><a href="/opinion/1778846/terrell-v-garcia/#127" aria-description="Citation for case: Terrell v. Garcia"><em>Terrell v Garcia, supra, </em>at 127-128</a></span>. In the main the damages for wrongful pregnancy have been established prior to the child’s birth, and, thus, any benefits received by the plaintiffs from raising a child are logically severable from damages already incurred. As the <em>Troppi </em>Court noted, "[t]he benefits rule allows flexibility in the case-by-case adjudication of the enormously varied claims which the widespread use of oral contraceptives portends”. <span class="citation" data-id="2185063"><a href="/opinion/2185063/troppi-v-scarf/#256" aria-description="Citation for case: Troppi v. Scarf">31 Mich App at 256</a></span>.</p>
<p id="b475-5">The dissent favors the tactic of the defense in the trial of this case which was supported by the trial court’s interpretation of <em>Troppi v <span class="citation" data-id="2185063"><a href="/opinion/2185063/troppi-v-scarf/" aria-description="Citation for case: Troppi v. Scarf">Scarf, supra.</a></span> </em>That benefit rule emerged from Restatement Torts, § 920, p 616 which provides:</p>
<blockquote id="b475-6">" 'Where the defendant’s tortious conduct has caused harm to the plaintiff or to his property and in so doing has conferred upon the plaintiff a special benefit to the interest which was harmed, the value of the benefit conferred is considered in mitigation of damages, where this is equitable.’ Restatement, Torts, § 920, p 616.” <span class="citation" data-id="2185063"><a href="/opinion/2185063/troppi-v-scarf/#254" aria-description="Citation for case: Troppi v. Scarf"><em>Troppi v Scarf, supra, </em>at 254</a></span>.</blockquote>
<p id="b475-7">We believe the last phrase of the quote from the Restatement is applicable here. To allow the defense to shield itself behind the love and affection of the plaintiffs for their healthy and lovable fifth child is less than equitable if indeed the plaintiffs’ claims are in fact true. That is, that the physician was negligent, that the plaintiff mother, a polio <page-number citation-index="1" label="464">*464</page-number>victim as. a child, had considerable and extreme difficulty with four previous deliveries, that she had a crippling condition of the spine, a significant difference in leg length, a distorted pelvis and had been subjected to extensive labor anywhere from 45 to 50 hours during delivery. Testimony indicates that plaintiff mother was placed under psychiatric care, threatening suicide shortly after her pregnancy was confirmed. Under these circumstances the damages claimed seem reasonably and equitably severable. The real damages are not child centered and the plaintiffs should not be forced to make fictional claims to suit the convenience of the defendants.<footnotemark>3</footnotemark> "The result we reach today is at best a mortal attempt to do justice in an imperfect world.” <em>Sherlock v Stillwater Clinic, </em><span class="citation" data-id="9723237"><a href="/opinion/2130993/sherlock-v-stillwater-clinic/" aria-description="Citation for case: Sherlock v. Stillwater Clinic">260 NW2d 169</a></span> (Minn, 1977).</p>
<p id="b476-6">Of course, our ruling does not establish that plaintiffs will ultimately be entitled to damages. Issues of contributory negligence and proximate cause, as well as damages, must await jury determination on proper instruction.</p>
<p id="b476-7">We reverse and remand for a new trial consistent with this opinion.</p>
